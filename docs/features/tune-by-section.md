# The tune by section

- **The tune by section** (branch `tune-by-section`, being tried; `melody/sections/`, state `state/SectionedText.svelte.js`; no buttons of its own, the user's call: it just happens): so the tune follows the chart when sections are cut, copied, reordered, renamed or dropped (the user's Logic step: copy a chorus, copy a verse as a starting point, drop a fiddle break). The tune and the edited bass line are each kept whole (ABC as typed or pasted, kept exactly: ABC users and tunes from the internet; and a transcription's tune, which follows its recording bar for bar) or by section (`tune` and `bassTune`: `{ header, voices, leads, parts, pickups }`, each section's bars in each voice by name; `abc` and `bass` still hold them as played). A tune drawn or started from the chart, and an arrangement made from a transcription, start by section (`Arranger.splitTuneIntoSections`, which leaves a transcription's tune whole). By section, `melody.notation` (and `bass.notation`) is the music as played, made in chart order (each voice through every section, a `P:` line per section, voices as the harmony writer lays them out), and every writer (the roll, harmony, a capo change, a respelling) is written back to the sections (`fromPlayedTune`; a changed `as` copy changes its source); the ABC view (`melody.abcView`) shows each section's tune once, its voices under it, and writes back the same way. A write that can't be read back keeps it whole, so nothing is lost. **Keys by section** (`melody/sections/sectionKeys.js`): a section's bars written in another key than the tune's own K: start with that `[K:]`, so the key travels with the section and a later chart key change never moves its pitches (only spelling); put together (as played, the ABC view) a section in the tune's own key after one in another gets the tune's key named again (`withKeyResets`), taken off on write-back (`withoutReset`); the split and a section's rests use the same rule (`withKeyFields`). `as` plays the source's tune, and so does a second line with the same name (a pasted copy); a section with nothing of its own plays rests with the chart's chords (the bass: the band's own line for those bars); words come from the chart per section as played (never kept with a tune). Adding or removing bars in a section adds or removes the same bars in its tune and bass line, in every voice (`melody/sections/barsFollowChart.js`: chart bars matched like a text diff, `bass/matchBars.js`, but only when a section's number of bars changed: a chord or time signature edited keeps every bar's notes, apart from a bar whose time signature changed (a chord change once looked like a bar removed and another added, and emptied that bar in every voice, fixed 2026-11-27); new bars get the rests a section with nothing written plays, `SectionedText.emptyBars`; a tie into a removed bar is let go; a section whose tune has repeats or endings, or bars that don't line up one for one, is left as it is; `state/tuneFollowsChart.svelte.js`, which follows only edits of the same song, `Arranger.songsLoaded`). Renaming a section moves its tune; renaming a copy (or writing chords into an `as` line) gives it its own copy of the tune to change from there (`renamedParts.js`, `state/tuneFollowsChart.svelte.js`). A dropped section's tune stays in the store, so pasting its line back brings it back. **Section pickups** (`sectionPickups.js`): a pickup belongs to the section it leads into (`pickups`: its length and its bar in each voice); the first section's is the song's pickup bar, and wherever a later one plays, its notes are laid over the last beats of whatever comes before, and lifted back out on write-back. They're set in Transcribe (a marker's "Tune starts … before (pickup)", below) and carried by Send to the arranger (`withPickupsCutOut`: the notes in those beats leave the section before, in every voice of the tune); a tune split in the arranger only gets its opening pickup. Pickups are the tune's only (the user's call): the chords, the band and the bass keep to the bar lines. Checked in the browser pane: arrangement from a transcription, reordering, renaming a copy, roll and ABC view edits, tenor harmony, `as`, words. Gaps: a section whose `as` copy differed (a second ending) loses the difference on split; chord changes inside an edited section don't rewrite its bass bars.
