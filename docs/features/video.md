# Video (A/V lanes)

- **An A/V lane** (Tracks, **+ A/V** beside + Audio; a camera mark in its header) is an audio lane that films as it records: the same record track, mixer channel, takes, clips and menu as any audio lane (`takes/recordTracks.js`). Which lanes film and how their tiles are laid out are saved with the song (`song.video`, `arranger/video/songVideo.js`). Started Nov 2026 on main, in slices: (1) record and line up, (2) the tiles, (3) following the tempo (done), (4) clips and storage.
- **The camera** (`CameraPicker`, beside the video; `settings.video.camera`, this device's): the front camera, or any the device lists; on a Mac that includes a nearby iPhone through **Continuity Camera** (macOS Ventura on: the browser sees it as an ordinary camera, so nothing special is needed; it's better than a laptop's and can be mounted anywhere). `video/camera.js` `cameraConstraints`. Not tried with a real iPhone yet.
- **Picture earlier / later** (`FilmNudge`, in the A/V lane's take list, 17 ms a tap, about a frame at 60 fps): the sound lines a film up exactly, but only against the _film's sound_, which is your mic; a wireless camera's picture can arrive late on its own, and only an eye (or, later, a clap seen and heard) can tell. Kept as the take's `video.nudgeMs`.
- **Recording**: one `getUserMedia` stream holds the mic and the camera (`audio/recording/micInput.js`'s `camera` option; an iPhone lets only one capture have the mic). The sound goes through the usual take recorder (full quality, on the band's sample clock); the film through MediaRecorder (`$lib/video/videoRecorder.js`), which starts before the count-in and stops after the take. **Capture format** (the user asked for one fit for this): front camera, 720p (`video/camera.js`: plenty for a tile, half the size of 1080p) at **60 fps** (a take slowed to half speed still shows 30), H.264 in MP4 where the browser can (every device plays it; WebM only as a fallback; `avc3` first, which carries the picture's settings with the frames, so a phone turned mid-take still records: Chrome warned that `avc1` can't; whether every device plays an avc3 file is to be checked when films are shared), 4 Mbit/s (fast fingers), a keyframe every second where the browser allows (Chrome; seeks stay quick and exact), the file's own sound at 64 kbit/s (only used to line up). About 30 MB a minute. While recording, the lane's tile shows the camera, mirrored; the film is kept the right way round.
- **One clock**: everything follows the audio context's clock. The take's sound is on it to the sample; the film is placed by finding the take's sound inside the film's own sound (`takes/filmOffset.js`, `audio/analysis/findOffset.js`: loudness envelopes matched over the whole span the take could be in, 10 ms steps then 1 ms; the whole take, because music repeats by the beat but a performance's dynamics don't). Saved as the take's `video.offset` (seconds into the film where the take's first sample is). The clocks alone were measured 0.6 s out in Chrome (MediaRecorder's start event isn't where its file starts), so they're only a fallback when the film's sound can't be decoded. Checked: Node (a strict 240 BPM beat, within 0.3 ms, 72 ms for a 3-minute take) and the browser pane with a stubbed camera and mic (the saved offset matched an independent scan).
- **Playing**: a film plays where its lane's clips say (`video/filmAt.js`: the same clips as the sound, so a trim or a comp cuts the film too), following where the band is **heard** (`video/bandHeardAt.js`: the band's position less the output delay, as the metronome does, so a film doesn't run ahead on Bluetooth). `video/followSong.js` (a Svelte action on the frame loop) pulls a small drift in by playing up to 5% faster or slower and seeks a big one; stopped, it shows the frame at the playhead; no filmed clip there, the tile is blank. Checked in the browser pane: one seek at the start, then within a few ms.
- **The frame** (the user's ask, Nov 2026: optimise for sharing reels on Facebook, as the default): the video is laid out in the finished video's shape, `$lib/video/frames.js` (data): **Reel 9:16, 1080×1920 (the default)**, Feed 4:5 (1080×1350), Wide 16:9; chosen per song (`song.video.frame`) in the shape menu. Research (Nov 2026): Meta's Reels docs recommend 1080×1920, minimum 540×960, a fixed 24–60 fps, H.264, AAC; Facebook shows reels at up to 720p (so 720p filming is enough); third-party guides say Facebook covers about the top 210 px (name) and bottom 290 px (caption, buttons) of a reel, not confirmed by Meta. Those bands are shaded on the frame (`covered`), to keep faces and hands out of them.
- **The tiles** fill the frame (`components/video/VideoWall.svelte`, each film cropped to fill its tile, `object-fit: cover`, as a reel would), so many across (`video/tileLayout.js`: every layout that saves a row), chosen with `VideoLayoutPicker` (keys picturing the grid in the frame's shape). **By default the layout that crops least** (`bestColumns`: tiles nearest the films' own shape, from each take's filmed size, `video.width`/`height`; before anything is filmed, the frame's shape): in a reel two players stack; four filmed upright make 2×2 tall tiles, filmed sideways four wide strips. Adding or removing an A/V lane goes back to the best layout. So: **film upright** (phone held tall) for reels. Under the tracks on Tracks (`VideoSection`, the frame up to 70% of the screen tall) and filling the **Video page** (`/arranger/video`, with the player bar: the band plays the whole song from the playhead, as on Tracks).
- **Following the tempo**: a film plays at its take's speed (`filmAt`'s `speed`, from the lane clip's stretch): at half the tempo the `<video>` plays at 0.5×, the drift pull-in on top. Checked in the browser pane: 0.500× at 119 for a take recorded at 238, 1× at 238. 60 fps filming keeps half speed smooth.
- Films are stored with the takes in IndexedDB (`storage/songTakes.js`, store `takeVideo`, read only when a tile needs one, played from an object URL let go with the song).
- Not yet: lining a picture up automatically (a clap seen and heard), films in shared songs (a received A/V take plays sound only), exporting one video of the tiles (the frame's size is the seam: 1080×1920 for a reel), storage beyond IndexedDB. Not tried on a real camera or an iPhone.
