# Transcribe a song

- **Transcribe a song** (`/arranger/transcribe`; Transcribe in the top bar, or the song bar's "New from…" menu, "A recording…"; a song written from a recording names it (`song.reference`: the recording's key and name), and arriving with one open reopens that recording if it's one of the app's own (`transcription/bundledReferences.js`, `reopenSongsReference`); a file from the device has to be picked again, and the page says so; logic `transcription/`, UI `components/transcription/`; a prototype, shaped with the user by mockups). Modelled on Logic after two "Learn a song" attempts were judged "nothing like Logic"; tapping along was tried and dropped (the user doesn't want tapping); a "Start a song from this recording" button and a Smart Tempo tab were judged weird and removed. **Desktop first** (the user's call): the phone gets its own layout later, so don't judge or tune the phone yet. The band's player bar and drawers are off here (`components/page/bandPages.js`) and arriving stops the arranger's band. **The band plays the chart along with the recording** (guitar, mandolin, bass: the arranger's own sound, through the mixer, at the recording's usual tempo, each note moved onto the recording's time through the tempo map, `transcription/bandAlong.js`): the stretch player reports which moment of the song is heard at which audio-clock time (`Clock`, about 40 times a second and at once after a jump), and each report queues the notes due in the next 0.2 s, so the band follows any speed, jumps and the cycle; a jump, pause or chart change stops the band and the next report makes a fresh one (the upright can't cancel queued notes). One **Band** lane (`TranscribeBand.svelte.js`; its lane is the chords by beat, below: a separate read-only Band lane drawing the same chords was merged into it); its **Guess** button (`GuessChordsButton.svelte`, `TranscriptionState.guessChords`) fills every bar with no chord yet with a guess from the recording, to fix by ear, and never touches chords you wrote (the user's call on 2026-11-21, reversing "no chord detection": a guess to correct, only when asked). `transcription/chords/`: `notesSounding.js` measures the 12 notes over a stretch in long frames at the band's own tuning (measured with `findTuning`, so it works before you tune), `guessChords.js` hears it the way bluegrass is played, in the band's own beat groups (`beatGroups`: 1–2 and 3–4 in 4/4, the whole bar in 3/4): the chops above the bass (200–1500 Hz) say which chord and whether it's minor, the boom's bass (40–200 Hz, long frames) backs up its root; on a bar's first boom the fifth counts a little, and inside a bar the chord carrying on is expected on its root or fifth while a new chord must arrive on its root (the bass walking to the fifth had read as C,G splits) and win by `MID_BAR_CHANGE_MARGIN` (0.25: the user's point that bluegrass isn't chordy, chords rarely change mid-bar); a new bar keeps the chord before unless another wins by `NEW_BAR_CHANGE_MARGIN` (0.05; 0.1 swallowed real D chords in Mighty Dark to Travel); the one common quick change, `| I V | I |` (the user's), needs only `QUICK_CHANGE_MARGIN` (0.08): the major chord a fifth up for the rest of the bar, when the next bar goes back home (so every bar is heard first, then decided, each looking at its neighbours; a bar next to one you wrote carries nothing over); minor is held back (`penalty` 0.1: blue thirds read as minor). Compared by script against a plainer first version (all notes, bass on beat 1) on four bundled songs: about as good, cleaner bar lines, fewer stray minors; no song with known chords to score against yet, and it depends on beat 1 being on the bar line. The chords it can guess are data (`QUALITIES`: major and minor so far; a 7th is one line). About 0.1 s a song. Names are plain (`Bb`, `F#`), not yet spelled for the key has its own volume, mute and solo (not saved, like the recording's); while the page is up its channels (`BAND_CHANNELS`) are kept out of the mixer's solo (`soloSafeWhile` in `mixer.svelte.js`), so soloing a part's lane (its mixer channel) doesn't silence the band: the lanes' solos (`laneLevels`) decide; each instrument's level is the mixer's (the user's call). A bass written by ear plays on the band's upright, not as tones, and its own lane's mute and solo count. For one clock, the stretch player runs in the native AudioContext under Tone's (`$lib/audio/nativeContext.js`: Tone's wrapper can't load a worklet with `import` lines, as Vite serves it in development). Checked in the browser pane by measuring Tone's output (boom-chucks on the chords, silent on N.C. and when muted, twice as far apart at 50%); not heard by the user, timing against the recording not judged by ear. **The parts you write play with the recording on the same sound a tapped note makes** (the user's call: one sound, the part's mixer instrument, the bass on the upright, a generic lane as the melody; `playback/partVoice.js` is the one place, used by `auditionNote` and by `transcription/TranscribeParts.svelte.js`, which queues them from the stretch player's clock reports through `bandAlong.js` like the band; plain tones in the worklet came before); `transcription/heardParts.js` moves the lanes' notes onto the recording's time and turns mute/solo into levels (a lane's level in front of its channel is only heard or not: its volume is its channel's fader). Like the band, a note in the first few hundredths of a second after a jump can be missed while the voices are made. **M/S and a volume on every lane** (the mixer's `MuteSoloButtons`; `LaneVolume.svelte`, a small fader across on the console's taper, so the part can be heard over a loud recording): a part's are its mixer channel's (as on the Tracks page; its volume is the channel's fader, saved with the mix), the Recording's its own (`referenceLane`, not saved); soloing any lane silences the rest, the recording included (`laneLevels` turns them into gains).   - **A transcription is a mockup tied to its recording; songs are made from its named sections** (the user's Logic workflow, and the user's rule that a section must have a defined start and end): a section exists only where you put a marker with its name; it ends at the next marker, an **End** marker ("End of section" in the bar menu, `SECTION_END` in `readChartOnBars.js`: the bars after it are in no section), or the recording's end. Bars in no section are written as `"Song"` in the transcription's chart (`UNMARKED_SECTION`) and never leave it. **Send to the arranger…** (top right, `components/transcription/SendToArranger.svelte`, `state/arrangeFromTranscription.js`, `transcription/arrangementFromTranscription.js`) needs the song's key named first (the user's call: the band needs it; the dialog asks for it with the same Key menu, and `arrangeFromTranscription` refuses without it; a capo is a guitar matter, to be handled later, not the band's), then asks what you want to do: **Practise a part** (one section, e.g. a mando break, as a song of its own, "<name> – Mandolin solo", opening on the chords like any song; the arranger loops it) or **Build an arrangement** (the sections you tick, in the recording's order, "<name> arrangement", rearranged in the arranger). The new song gets the chords, words, the song's time and key, its tune and written bass cut by section, the transcription's tempo and mix, no recording; the transcription is left as it is. **Back and forth**: the new song remembers the recording it came from (`madeFrom`; not `reference`, which means the song is the transcription itself, whose chart Transcribe writes on the recording's bars).
