# Transcribe a song

- **Transcribe a song** (`/arranger/transcribe`; Transcribe in the top bar, or the song bar's "New from…" menu, "A recording…"; a song written from a recording names it (`song.reference`: the recording's key and name), and arriving with one open reopens that recording if it's one of the app's own (`transcription/bundledReferences.js`, `reopenSongsReference`); a file from the device has to be picked again, and the page says so; logic `transcription/`, UI `components/transcription/`; a prototype, shaped with the user by mockups). Modelled on Logic after two "Learn a song" attempts were judged "nothing like Logic"; tapping along was tried and dropped (the user doesn't want tapping); a "Start a song from this recording" button and a Smart Tempo tab were judged weird and removed. **Desktop first** (the user's call): the phone gets its own layout later, so don't judge or tune the phone yet. The band's player bar and the mixer drawer are off here (`components/page/bandPages.js`, which lists each page's drawers) and arriving stops the arranger's band. **The pencil** on the control bar (once the song is this recording's) opens the editor drawer, as on Tracks: the chart as text (the quickest way to sketch a form and name sections; the grid follows as you type, since the chart is the one place chords and sections live) and the tune as ABC (its Melody tab). **While the recording plays, the bar being heard is lit in the chart's text** (red, in the Band lane's editor and the drawer's; the band's playback lights it the same way on every page): `chart/barSpan.js` finds the bar's characters (a repeat's bar where it's written), `TranscriptionState.chartBarAtPlayhead` says which bar, through `Arranger.chartBarNow()`, which also answers for the band on the Arranger and Tracks pages (the same editor, in the Edit drawer; Transcribe's recording only while its page is open, `onPage`). Paused, the bar stays lit where the playhead rests (the band's: where the next Play carries on, so none with Loop on, and none at the start); the editor lays a copy of the text under the textarea, which goes see-through while a bar is lit, and keeps the bar in view unless you're typing. **The band plays the chart along with the recording** (guitar, mandolin, bass: the arranger's own sound, through the mixer, at the recording's usual tempo, each note moved onto the recording's time through the tempo map, `transcription/bandAlong.js`): the stretch player reports which moment of the song is heard at which audio-clock time (`Clock`, about 40 times a second and at once after a jump), and each report queues the notes due in the next 0.2 s, so the band follows any speed, jumps and the cycle; a jump, pause or chart change stops the band and the next report makes a fresh one (the upright can't cancel queued notes). One **Band** lane (`TranscribeBand.svelte.js`; its lane is the chords by beat, below: a separate read-only Band lane drawing the same chords was merged into it); its **Guess** button (`GuessChordsButton.svelte`, `TranscriptionState.guessChords`, below; nothing is guessed until asked, the user's call on 2026-11-21, reversing "no chord detection": a guess to correct) fills the bars with chords from the recording, to fix by ear; once any bar has a chord the button is **Clear chords** (`clearChords`), which empties every bar, typed ones too (the user's call: a plain toggle, not tracking which were guessed). Its notes are counted with each frequency's level square-rooted (`notesSounding.js`), so a loud voice or fiddle doesn't drown the strum: on a synthetic band with known chords (a scratch script: boom-chuck, a wandering voice with blue notes, hiss) that took it from 89% to 97% of beat groups right, and on the bundled songs chords outside the key's I, IV and V fell from 12% to 7% (counting only spectral peaks, a cube root or a log were each worse). Guessing the sections (first from the repeating chord cycle, then from where the recording's sound changes, which matched True Life Blues' 16-bar cycles but drifted on Poison Love) and the bass line (a note per boom) were built and removed on 2026-11-22: the user found them not good enough to be worth having. `transcription/chords/`: `notesSounding.js` measures the 12 notes over a stretch in long frames at the band's own tuning (measured with `findTuning`, so it works before you tune), `guessChords.js` hears it the way bluegrass is played, in the band's own beat groups (`beatGroups`: 1–2 and 3–4 in 4/4, the whole bar in 3/4): the chops above the bass (200–1500 Hz) say which chord and whether it's minor, the boom's bass (40–200 Hz, long frames) backs up its root; on a bar's first boom the fifth counts a little, and inside a bar the chord carrying on is expected on its root or fifth while a new chord must arrive on its root (the bass walking to the fifth had read as C,G splits) and win by `MID_BAR_CHANGE_MARGIN` (0.25: the user's point that bluegrass isn't chordy, chords rarely change mid-bar); a new bar keeps the chord before unless another wins by `NEW_BAR_CHANGE_MARGIN` (0.05; 0.1 swallowed real D chords in Mighty Dark to Travel); the one common quick change, `| I V | I |` (the user's), needs only `QUICK_CHANGE_MARGIN` (0.08): the major chord a fifth up for the rest of the bar, when the next bar goes back home (so every bar is heard first, then decided, each looking at its neighbours; a bar next to one you wrote carries nothing over); minor is held back (`penalty` 0.1: blue thirds read as minor). Compared by script against a plainer first version (all notes, bass on beat 1) on four bundled songs: about as good, cleaner bar lines, fewer stray minors; scored on 2026-11-22 against two songs the user corrected by ear (Poison Love, True Life Blues; a dev Save test button and `scripts/scoreChordGuesses.js` did it, removed once the guesser was good enough, the user's call): Poison Love, key C: 95% of half bars right, 94% without the key, 85% before the square root below; its misses are mostly a change caught a bar late or early), and **once you've said the key it favours the key's own chords** (`theory/bluegrassChords.js`, data; the user's rule: bluegrass is mostly diatonic, so any other chord is suspect, not wrong: the diatonic chords are at home, ♭VII, II (V of V), III (on to IV) and VI (Salty Dog) a little suspect, anything else, a minor on I, IV or V included, unlikely, since the players' bluesy flat third over a major chord mustn't read as minor); it removed Poison Love's stray B♭ and E and took the synthetic songs from 97% to 98%. A functional reading (does an E really lead to Am?) wasn't needed yet, and it depends on beat 1 being on the bar line. The chords it can guess are data (`QUALITIES`: major and minor so far; a 7th is one line). About 0.1 s a song. Names are plain (`Bb`, `F#`), not yet spelled for the key has its own volume, mute and solo (not saved, like the recording's); while the page is up its channels (`BAND_CHANNELS`) are kept out of the mixer's solo (`soloSafeWhile` in `mixer.svelte.js`), so soloing a part's lane (its mixer channel) doesn't silence the band: the lanes' solos (`laneLevels`) decide; each instrument's level is the mixer's (the user's call). A bass written by ear plays on the band's upright, not as tones, and its own lane's mute and solo count. For one clock, the stretch player runs in the native AudioContext under Tone's (`$lib/audio/nativeContext.js`: Tone's wrapper can't load a worklet with `import` lines, as Vite serves it in development). Checked in the browser pane by measuring Tone's output (boom-chucks on the chords, silent on N.C. and when muted, twice as far apart at 50%); not heard by the user, timing against the recording not judged by ear. **The parts you write play with the recording on the same sound a tapped note makes** (the user's call: one sound, the part's mixer instrument, the bass on the upright, a generic lane as the melody; `playback/partVoice.js` is the one place, used by `auditionNote` and by `transcription/TranscribeParts.svelte.js`, which queues them from the stretch player's clock reports through `bandAlong.js` like the band; plain tones in the worklet came before); `transcription/heardParts.js` moves the lanes' notes onto the recording's time and turns mute/solo into levels (a lane's level in front of its channel is only heard or not: its volume is its channel's fader). Like the band, a note in the first few hundredths of a second after a jump can be missed while the voices are made. **M/S and a volume on every lane** (the mixer's `MuteSoloButtons`; `LaneVolume.svelte`, a small fader across on the console's taper, so the part can be heard over a loud recording): a part's are its mixer channel's (as on the Tracks page; its volume is the channel's fader, saved with the mix), the Recording's its own (`referenceLane`, not saved); soloing any lane silences the rest, the recording included (`laneLevels` turns them into gains). The top bar's Transcribe is still the older slow-downer page (`/transcribe`).
  - **Opening a recording goes straight to the workspace** (the user's call, 2026-11-21: placing beat 1 on a step of its own, with the time, key and song name, felt wrong; everything moved into the workspace, Logic's way). `TranscribeSetup.svelte` only chooses the recording ("Which recording do you want to work out?") and shows "Listening to the recording…" while its beats are found (`setupStep`: `'reference' | 'loading' | null`). Earlier tries, all dropped: a three-step wizard, a beat 1 step with a tap-along "Beat 1 is here", Listen / Sounds right, a Pickup menu, and name and key required before the workspace.
    1. **The beats are found at once** (not the bars): `detectBeats(analysis)` tracks from the recording's strongest hit, forward and back. The grid starts with bar 1 on the first beat and the whole recording used.
    2. **The time signature lays out the bars** (`ChooseTime.svelte`: big buttons, each with its count and what it's for, shown above the grid until chosen; then `TimeSignatureSelect.svelte`, Time, in the song row). Until it's chosen the grid shows only the beats and the recording: no ruler bars, cycle, Band or part lanes. Choosing it the first time makes the song (`startSong`: named for the recording, rename it in the song bar, which shows only once this recording's song is open); changing it later lays the bars out again from bar 1 (odd bars are forgotten). `timeChosen` is kept in the draft.
    3. **Slide the recording onto the bars** (`ReferenceLane.svelte`, like a Logic region): drag its top strip (the recording's name) and it moves a beat at a time, its beats with it (`TranscriptionState.slidePreview` while dragging, `slideBy` on letting go, `beatGrid.js` `withSlide`: bar 1 moves to another of its beats). **A pickup is bar 1** (the user's call): put the song's first ONE on bar 2; bar 1 is a real bar of the chart (N.C. until it has a chord) and the tune's notes go in it. Its section's marker goes on bar 2 with "Tune starts … before (pickup)", and Send carries those notes over, as for any section; the first bar has no pickup choice. Bar 1 may start up to 4 bars before the first beat found (`MOST_BARS_BEFORE`): such beats are carried on from the beats beside them (`beatSeconds`, `tempoMap.js` `secondsAtBeat`), so code reads a bar's time through those, never `beats[index]`. What of the recording lies before bar 1 is shaded and never played.
    4. **Trim it** by its ends (`withTrim`, `grid.trim` in seconds, at least a second long): the beats outside aren't shown, no bars are laid past the end (`gridBars`), Play starts no earlier than the trim's start (`startSeconds`) and stops at its end (`stopAtTheEnd`, from the page). An intro or false start (Poison Love's) is trimmed off, or left in as bars before the song's first ONE.
       Slides and trims go through the grid's undo (⌘Z) like any grid change. **Key** (`TranscriptionKeySelect.svelte`) is in the song row too, only needed before Send to the arranger (which asks for it). Checked in the browser pane (desktop, Poison Love): beats on opening, 4/4 lays the bars out and makes the song, sliding by two beats and ⌘Z, trimming both ends, Play from the trimmed start and stopping at the trimmed end; phone width doesn't widen the page. Not by ear, not on a phone. Not built: a guess of where ONE is (the user's call: no guessing it), nudging by less than a beat (the beats themselves are fixed in the Beats row), a phone layout for the grips.
  - **A transcription is a mockup tied to its recording; songs are made from its named sections** (the user's Logic workflow, and the user's rule that a section must have a defined start and end): a section exists only where you put a marker with its name; it ends at the next marker, an **End** marker ("End of section" in the bar menu, `SECTION_END` in `readChartOnBars.js`: the bars after it are in no section), or the recording's end. Bars in no section are written as `"Song"` in the transcription's chart (`UNMARKED_SECTION`) and never leave it. **Send to the arranger…** (top right, `components/transcription/SendToArranger.svelte`, `state/arrangeFromTranscription.js`, `transcription/arrangementFromTranscription.js`) needs the song's key named first (the user's call: the band needs it; the dialog asks for it with the same Key menu, and `arrangeFromTranscription` refuses without it; a capo is a guitar matter, to be handled later, not the band's), then asks what you want to do: **Practise a part** (one section, e.g. a mando break, as a song of its own, "<name> – Mandolin solo", opening on the chords like any song; the arranger loops it) or **Build an arrangement** (the sections you tick, in the recording's order, "<name> arrangement", rearranged in the arranger). Each shows its bars. With nothing named it says how to name sections. **A section's tune can start early** (a pickup, the tune only): a named marker's menu has "Tune starts: on the bar / 1–3 beats before (pickup)" (`setSectionPickup`; kept in the draft as `pickups`, bar number → beats; on the first bar it's the tune's own pickup before bar 1, the same as the roll's Pickup menu); the ruler says "Verse (pickup 1)", and Send moves those notes from the end of the section before into the section's pickup, so dropping or moving the section before never loses them. Two sections with the same name become "Verse", "Verse 2" (in an arrangement a repeated name repeats the first). The new song gets the chords, words, the song's time and key, its tune and written bass cut by section, the transcription's tempo and mix, no recording; the transcription is left as it is. **Back and forth**: the new song remembers the recording it came from (`madeFrom`; not `reference`, which means the song is the transcription itself, whose chart Transcribe writes on the recording's bars). Opening Transcribe with it open opens that recording and its own song (`TranscriptionState.openWhatItWasMadeFrom`) and offers **‹ Back to <name>** (`returnTo`), which leaves for the arranger first and then opens the song (opening it on the Transcribe page would bring its transcription straight back).
  - **The features work together and alone** (the user's model): one song, and tools that each use only their part (jam over a chart, build speed on a tune, transcribe a recording into a play-along, print a chart). Nothing may be assumed: no invented key, chord or bass.
  - **Opening a recording opens its song**: the song written from it (it names its recording, `song.reference`), found among your songs and opened, else a new song on its bars at its tempo (`TranscriptionState.startSong`, named for the recording and saved at once; the open song is already saved). If another song is open, a small "Use it for a new song" offers it. **The chart is the one place the chords, sections and key live** (the user's rule: the arranger and Transcribe are always in sync): Transcribe reads them from the chart on the recording's bars (`transcription/readChartOnBars.js`: the chart's bars in play order line up with the recording's, a section is a marker, its words are kept by bar), a chord typed here is written into its own bar of the chart in place (`chart/withBarChords.js`, so words, repeats, marks and fields stay), and a change to the bars, time or markers writes the chart again on the new bars (`chartFromBars.js`, keeping the words; repeats are written out and bars past the recording's end dropped). Nothing is written while the chart has an error.

  - **Finding beats and bars** (`transcription/beats/`, about 1–2 s on the main thread, once per recording per device: the analysis is kept in IndexedDB, `storage/referenceAnalyses.js`, stamped with `ANALYSIS_VERSION` and the audio's length and sample rate; raise the version when the analysis changes): `analyseReference.js` (onset strength, bass-only onsets, chroma), `estimateTempo.js` (autocorrelation leaning to 200 BPM, 40–360), `trackBeats.js` (Ellis's dynamic programming; beats may drift; tracked on the hits and the bass notes together, `hitsAndBass` in `detectBeats.js`: Poison Love's soft first bars had weak hits but clear booms, and on hits alone it counted 9 beats for 8, so bar 2 started a beat early), then tracked again held to the tempo of the 16 beats around each moment (`localTempo.js`: a band drifts but doesn't jump, so a finder that loses its place is carried through; this fixed On and On's bars 104–107, where the first run swallowed a beat), `detectBeats.js` (**counts every strum, like the chart: boom on 1, chuck on 2**; if strong hits still sit halfway between most beats, tracks again at half the gap), (`findDownbeat.js`, which guessed ONE from chord changes and bass, is gone: the user places it.) The first finder counted every other strum on 5 of the 8 bundled songs and sat on the chucks on 2; now all 8 have strong hits on the beats and the bass on them; ONE agrees between the song's halves on 5 of 8. Not compared with ground truth yet: Logic's tempo map for a few songs (a MIDI export after Smart Tempo) would be the answer key; Beat This! (neural; check licence) is the fallback.
