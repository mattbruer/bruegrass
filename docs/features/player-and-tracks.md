# Player bar, tempo and tracks

- **The player bar: as few controls as possible, each big and obvious** (the user's taste: the first iPhone's one button; design for a child, an elderly half-blind user, anyone non-technical). Raised keys, pictures only (names are in the tooltip and for screen readers): Play/Stop, To the start (with Loop off), Metronome, Tempo (− number +, a wide tile: `bar-tile wide`; on a phone the picture keys shrink to 40 px so all fit across 375 px), Edit, Mixer. Record is on the Tracks page, not the bar. Which tiles show is one list, `components/page/playerBarTiles.js` (it becomes one list per mode when the bar's contents change with the work, e.g. recording); how a tile looks (`bar-tile`: size, raised key, sinks when pressed or on) is in one place, `PlayerBar.svelte`. Fold related settings into one tile's panel rather than adding tiles: the metronome key (`components/player/MetronomeTile.svelte`) arms the click: lit, it clicks along with the band whenever it plays, following the song's bars, silent while stopped (`MetronomeState`, `settings.metronome.withBand`, this device's habit). The tempo is a tile of its own right beside it (`components/player/TempoTile.svelte`, always visible, never in a menu, because it's used all the time): − and + either side of the number (hold to repeat), the number typed in directly; a change restarts a playing band once it has rested (`components/player/restartWhenSettled.svelte.js`, as swing does). Its ⋯ key opens `TempoPanel.svelte`: swing, and the click's sound and level, saved in `settings.metronome`. A click on its own is the Metronome page's (below). **Count-in is only for recording** (the user's call, like Logic's default: a 1234 key on the player bar that counted in Play too was tried and taken off, commit 022f85e has it; its clicks clashed with the band's click when Play carried on mid-song). How many bars is the Count-in menu beside Record on the Tracks page (`settings.metronome.countInBars`). The count-in itself is the player's (`PlayerState.play`'s `countIn`, which recording turns on): `playback/metronome/countIn.js` says how many clicks (every beat of the bars; a pickup is sung over the last of them, the clicks carry on under it: the user's call), `playCountIn.js` plays them on the metronome's channel in its own sound, and `PlayerState.countInAt` says when it began (the recorder starts there). The band's click starts when the band comes in (`clicks.from`), never over the count-in. The tile pictures (`components/icons/`: `PlayIcon`, `StopIcon`, `MetronomeIcon`, `PencilIcon`, `SlidersIcon`) are detailed illustrations with their own colours (the one exception to theme colours), and each animates while hovered with a mouse, or once when tapped on a phone (`components/page/animateKeys.js` marks the key; each picture's CSS says how it moves; not with reduced motion): notes float off Play, the metronome swings at the tempo, the pencil draws a loop, the faders ride. The metronome's weight sits higher for slower tempos, and its pendulum swings on the click (`state/MetronomeState.svelte.js` `beatsNow()`, `playback/metronome/swing.js`). Leaving the page stops the music and the click.
- **Tracks** (`/arranger/tracks`, the Tracks link beside Chart; logic `tracks/`, UI `components/tracks/`): where the DAW side of the app grows (the user's call, Nov 2026). The song as lanes on one timeline, laid out like Transcribe (the user's call): a sticky control bar (`TracksControls.svelte`: Play/Stop (Space), back to the start, the clock (`PlayClock`, bars and beats), **Record** with its count-in, the click and the tempo (`TempoStepper`), Zoom, `settings.tracks.zoom`), then the grid (`TracksView.svelte`, Transcribe's look: raised lane headers with mute, solo and volume). **No player bar here** (`bandPages.js`): the page has its own controls, and Record lives here only (it was taken off the arranger's player bar to make room for the tempo). Differences from Transcribe: no reference recording, and a **steady tempo**: the ruler is the song's bars at its BPM (`tracks/ruler.js`, from the count-in bar, where pickups and take files start), not a tempo map. No piano roll, and no + Lane for notes. The band plays the **whole song once from the playhead**, whatever the arranger's loop (`LoopState.playThroughWhile`, released when you leave; the arranger's loop is kept); click the ruler to move the playhead (`PlayerState.goTo`). Lanes are plain data (`tracks/lanes.js`, id = mixer channel): band lanes are blocks, chords for Guitar and Mandolin, notes by pitch for Bass, Melody and each harmony part; **tap a chord block to change it** (the first piece of the arranger coming over to Tracks, the user's thinking being the two pages may become one): each chord block carries where it's written in the chart (`place`, `chordBlocks` in `tracks/lanes.js`), and the tap opens the same edit box and chord tutor as the arranger's strip (`chordEdit/ChordDialogs.svelte`, shared by both), so a chord in a section played twice changes both times. The blocks show the chord as written (the shapes), not the strip's chord display, and only rename a chord: splitting a bar or moving a change is still the chart editor's (Transcribe's Band lane has a box per beat, a model for that); **audio lanes** are the four record tracks (`takes/recordTracks.js`), shown while a track has takes or has been added: **+ Audio lane** names the next free track (`NameDialog`, the name kept in the song's `partNames`, so an empty lane is saved with the song) and arms it. Each audio lane's header has a record button (Logic's record-enable: Record records on the armed lane, `RecordingState.armedTrack`, the first lane if none is armed) and a ⋯ menu (`AudioLaneMenu.svelte`: rename, its takes via `TakeList`, a take from a file, Remove for an empty lane). What recording has to say (problems, the mic and its delay, Bluetooth) is under the controls (`RecordingNotes.svelte`). The take in use is drawn as a clip with its waveform (`takes/waveformPeaks.js`, cached per take in `tracks/takeWaveforms.js`); drag it sideways to line it up (`tracks/dragSideways.js`, writes `nudgeMs`), tap it for the lane's next take. The playhead follows `player.melodyTimeNow` (`tracks/followPlayhead.js`). Desktop first, like Transcribe: on a phone the controls wrap and the grid scrolls sideways, but the 186 px lane headers leave little room. Not yet: cycle, bar back/forward, input monitoring, choosing the input, more than four audio lanes. Not tried on a real iPhone yet.
