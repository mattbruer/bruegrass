# Player bar, tempo and tracks

- **The player bar: as few controls as possible, each big and obvious** (the user's taste: the first iPhone's one button; design for a child, an elderly half-blind user, anyone non-technical). Raised keys, pictures only (names are in the tooltip and for screen readers): Play/Stop, To the start (with Loop off), Metronome, Tempo (− number +, a wide tile: `bar-tile wide`; on a phone the picture keys shrink to 40 px so all fit across 375 px), Edit, Mixer. Record is on the Tracks page, not the bar. Which tiles show is one list, `components/page/playerBarTiles.js` (it becomes one list per mode when the bar's contents change with the work, e.g. recording); how a tile looks (`bar-tile`: size, raised key, sinks when pressed or on) is in one place, `PlayerBar.svelte`. Fold related settings into one tile's panel rather than adding tiles: the metronome key (`components/player/MetronomeTile.svelte`) arms the click: lit, it clicks along with the band whenever it plays, following the song's bars, silent while stopped (`MetronomeState`, `settings.metronome.withBand`, this device's habit). The tempo is a tile of its own right beside it (`components/player/TempoTile.svelte`, always visible, never in a menu, because it's used all the time): − and + either side of the number (hold to repeat), the number typed in directly; a change restarts a playing band once it has rested (`components/player/restartWhenSettled.svelte.js`, as swing does). Its ⋯ key opens `TempoPanel.svelte`: swing, and the click's sound and level, saved in `settings.metronome`. A click on its own is the Metronome page's (below). **Count-in is only for recording** (the user's call, like Logic's default: a 1234 key on the player bar that counted in Play too was tried and taken off, commit 022f85e has it; its clicks clashed with the band's click when Play carried on mid-song). How many bars is the Count-in menu beside Record on the Tracks page (`settings.metronome.countInBars`). The count-in itself is the player's (`PlayerState.play`'s `countIn`, which recording turns on): `playback/metronome/countIn.js` says how many clicks (every beat of the bars; a pickup is sung over the last of them, the clicks carry on under it: the user's call), `playCountIn.js` plays them on the metronome's channel in its own sound, and `PlayerState.countInAt` says when it began (the recorder starts there). The band's click starts when the band comes in (`clicks.from`), never over the count-in. The tile pictures (`components/icons/`: `PlayIcon`, `StopIcon`, `MetronomeIcon`, `PencilIcon`, `SlidersIcon`) are detailed illustrations with their own colours (the one exception to theme colours), and each animates while hovered with a mouse, or once when tapped on a phone (`components/page/animateKeys.js` marks the key; each picture's CSS says how it moves; not with reduced motion): notes float off Play, the metronome swings at the tempo, the pencil draws a loop, the faders ride. The metronome's weight sits higher for slower tempos, and its pendulum swings on the click (`state/MetronomeState.svelte.js` `beatsNow()`, `playback/metronome/swing.js`). Leaving the page stops the music and the click.
- **Tracks** (`/arranger/tracks`, the Tracks link beside Chart; logic `tracks/`, UI `components/tracks/`): where the DAW side of the app grows (the user's call, Nov 2026). The song as lanes on one timeline, laid out like Transcribe (the user's call): a sticky control bar (`TracksControls.svelte`: Play/Stop (Space), back to the start, the clock (`PlayClock`, bars and beats), **Record** with its count-in, the click and the tempo (`TempoStepper`), **Edit** and **the Mixer** (the arranger's editor and mixer drawers, coming over as Tracks takes over from the arranger page; on this page they sit on the bottom of the screen, `bandPages.js` `showsTheDrawers`; their keys are `components/page/DrawerKey.svelte`, and where they live may change once the page's layout settles), Undo, Zoom, `settings.tracks.zoom`), then the grid (`TracksView.svelte`, Transcribe's look: raised lane headers with mute, solo and volume). **No player bar here** (`bandPages.js`): the page has its own controls, and Record lives here only (it was taken off the arranger's player bar to make room for the tempo). Differences from Transcribe: no reference recording, and the ruler is the song's bars (`tracks/ruler.js`, from the count-in bar, where pickups and take files start), evenly spaced whatever the tempo, not a tempo map. **The tempo lane** is under the ruler (the piano roll's, `components/tempo/TempoLane.svelte`, shared: the host says where a place is across and snaps the pointer, here to the beat): draw a ritard there too; audio is placed through the song's clock (`tracks/audioScale.js`), so in a ritard a clip's audio sits under the slower bars. **Editing a part** (a sketch to try, the user's call after weighing a page against a pane, Nov 2026): a part's lane (Bass, Melody, harmony and added parts; its name, or a click on the lane) opens its editor under the lanes, `components/tracks/PartPane.svelte` (Transcribe's bottom pane, moved and shared: the host says which part, what it follows and where the view choice is kept, `settings.tracks.partView`): the piano roll, score or TAB, on the band's clock (`player.melodyTimeNow`, a click on the roll's ruler moves the playhead), so the band keeps playing while you edit. The open part is the page's history state (SvelteKit shallow routing: `pushState`, switching parts replaces it), so Back or an iPhone swipe closes it, as does its ✕; opening one first clears the entry it opens from, because after a reload that entry still holds an editor that isn't open (SvelteKit refuses `replaceState` while the page starts, which breaks the router, so it can't be cleared on load). It scrolls into view below the sticky control bar (`TracksControls`'s `height`). On a phone it stays a pane under the lanes for now (the user's call, Nov 2026, judged in the simulator only; the control bar takes a third of the screen, so a full-screen sheet with Done may come back after trying a real iPhone). The pane is one component and the page decides where it sits, so a sheet is a change to the page only. Not tried yet: the song-level writing (chart, words, ABC) staying on the arranger page. The band plays the **whole song once from the playhead**, whatever the arranger's loop (`LoopState.playThroughWhile`, released when you leave; the arranger's loop is kept), unless **the cycle** is on: as in Transcribe (the shared `components/tracks/CycleStrip.svelte` and `CycleSectionMenu.svelte`, moved there from Transcribe; drag across the strip above the ruler, the Cycle button, its ▾ for a section, C), but its ends snap to bar lines (`fit={onBarLines}`, `transcription/cycle.js`), because the band loops whole bars: `state/TracksCycleState.svelte.js` (made afresh each visit, not saved) turns the region into bar numbers (`cycleBarNumbers`), and the page hands them to `LoopState.cycleBars`, which loops them while playing through (`repeats`, `sectionsToPlay`), restarting a playing band on a change. With the cycle on, Play starts at the cycle's start (the arranger's loop always plays from its top; Transcribe carries on from a playhead inside the cycle); click the ruler to move the playhead (`PlayerState.goTo`). Lanes are plain data (`tracks/lanes.js`, id = mixer channel), in two kinds: **instrument lanes** (anything edited with the piano roll, and the band's chord lanes) and **audio lanes** (what the mic captured). **Their order**: instruments above audio until you move them (`tracks/laneOrder.js` `orderedLanes`; a lane not in your order yet joins its own group: an instrument lane after the last instrument, an audio lane at the bottom; a removed lane leaves the order, `PartsState.forget`); drag a lane by its grip (⠿, pointer events with `touch-action: none`, so the same on a phone and a mouse; a line shows where it drops, `dropIndex`), or Move up / Move down in its ⋯ menu (`MoveLane.svelte`, for the keyboard, or tapping); saved with the song (`song.laneOrder`, `PartsState.setOrder`). **Every lane can be renamed** from its ⋯ menu (instrument lanes: `InstLaneMenu.svelte`; Guitar and Mandolin too, through `partNames`, so the mixer follows). **+ Inst** (beside + Audio) adds a part to write with the piano roll, named first (`NameDialog`): an extra part of the tune, as Transcribe's + Lane (`state/PartsState.svelte.js`: adding, naming, removing, Make it the bass line, the order; Transcribe's lane methods call it), and opens its editor; its lane shows while it's still empty (the lanes follow the tune's voices, `voiceChannels`). Band lanes are blocks, chords for Guitar and Mandolin, notes by pitch for Bass, Melody and each harmony part; **the Guitar and Mandolin lanes are the chords by beat, typed like Transcribe's Band lane** (the first piece of the arranger coming over to Tracks: the user thinks the two pages may become one): the same component, `components/tracks/ChordLane.svelte` (moved there from Transcribe; the host gives it its bars, how to read a bar's slots and how to write them), and the same writer, `chartWithTypedBar` in `transcription/barChords.js` (the bar rewritten in place in the chart, `chart/withBarChords.js`), on the chart read as slots per bar (`readChartOnBars`). A chord typed in a section played twice changes both times. **A chord edit changes the chart, and the rest follows it**: the edited bass line (`bassFollowsChart`) and the tune's chord symbols (`chordsFollowChart`, see the melody doc). The lanes show chords as written (the shapes), not the strip's chord display; **audio lanes** are the four record tracks (`takes/recordTracks.js`), shown while a track has takes or has been added: **+ Audio** names the next free track (`NameDialog`, the name kept in the song's `partNames`, so an empty lane is saved with the song) and arms it. Each audio lane's header has a record button (Logic's record-enable: Record records on the armed lane, `RecordingState.armedTrack`, the first lane if none is armed) and a ⋯ menu (`AudioLaneMenu.svelte`: rename, Move up / down, its takes via `TakeList`, a take from a file, Remove for an empty lane). What recording has to say (problems, the mic and its delay, Bluetooth) is under the controls (`RecordingNotes.svelte`). **Audio editing** (the bread and butter, not a DAW; the user's call, Nov 2026): an audio lane plays **clips**, each a window onto a take (`takes/clips.js`: `{ takeId, from, to, shift, gain, fadeIn, fadeOut }`), saved with the song as `song.audioClips` by record track; a lane not in it plays its take in use whole, so nothing is saved until you edit. Edits never touch the audio. A clip sits where its take was recorded (the take's start with its latency and by-ear nudge, so Earlier/Later in the lane menu still moves all its clips) moved by its own `shift`. Drawn by `TakeClip.svelte` with the take's waveform for its window (`takes/waveformPeaks.js`, cached per take in `tracks/takeWaveforms.js`), taller when turned up. **Tap or click a clip to pick it** (`state/AudioEditsState.svelte.js`, one picked clip): it gets trim grips on its edges and fade squares at its top corners, and its tools show in the control bar under the transport (`ClipTools.svelte`, so they stay in view on a phone): **◂ Take n ▸** plays the same stretch of the song from another take of the lane, in time as that take was recorded (comping; a take that doesn't cover it is skipped), **Split** at the playhead (S; tap the ruler to put the playhead there: no precision tapping on a waveform), **Split at sections** (ready to comp section by section), **Level** (−24 to +12 dB) and **Even it out** (normalize to −1 dBFS, `takes/clipLevel.js`), **Delete** (Delete/Backspace; the stretch is silent; a lane with no clips says how to get them back), **Done** (Esc, or tap the empty lane). Dragging: a mouse drags any clip sideways (it moves, free, never snapped: lining audio up with the band is by ear); a finger drags only a picked clip, so a swipe over a clip still scrolls the lanes (`dragSideways`'s `touchDrags`); grips and fade squares have finger-sized targets. Trims and moves are free too (snapping to beats is a seam in `audioScale.secondsAt` if wanted). **Every edge fades** at least 8 ms (`EDGE_FADE`) so a cut never clicks; drawn fades are on top. Clips play through native nodes, a buffer source and a gain carrying the level and fades (`takes/playTakes.js` `gainEnvelope`), on the track's channel. Choosing a take in the lane menu, recording a new one or adding one from a file makes the lane play that take whole again (its clips are forgotten, as an edit Undo can take back). Overlapping clips on one lane both play. **Takes follow the tempo** (Nov 2026): a take keeps its own times (its start, its clips' edges, moves and fades, in seconds at the tempo it was recorded at, `take.bpm`) and `takes/takeTempo.js` is the one place that turns them into song time now (`stretchOf`: 2 at half the tempo; exact while the tempo lane is the same, since it bends by shares of the tempo; a tempo lane drawn after recording isn't followed). The lanes give song time now (`LaneClip.start`/`end`, and its `stretch`), so they're drawn on the same bars at any tempo; `AudioEditsState` turns drags and the playhead back into the take's own time; comping between takes recorded at different tempos matches them by the song (`fromTake`). A take at its own tempo plays through a plain buffer source, untouched and free; at another it plays through a **stretched source** (`$lib/audio/stretch/stretchSource.js`, the `stretch-source` worklet: the slow-downer's phase vocoder, started on an exact audio-clock time, one node per clip played; each take's audio is sent to the audio thread once and kept there by take id, forgotten when another song opens). It runs in the native context under Tone's (`nativeContextOf`; Tone's wrapper can't load a worklet with imports) and plays into the clip's level gain through `nativeNodeOf`, so it stays in the mixer. Checked in the browser pane: a 440 Hz take recorded at 238 played at 119 came out at 439.5 Hz (pitch kept), at speed 0.5, scheduled on the clock; at 238 no stretcher is made. **Cost**: measured in Node, about 0.1 s of CPU per second of output per stretched lane, so four stretched lanes take about 43% of one core of a Mac on the audio thread. Not measured on an iPhone: if it can't keep up, the fallback is stretching each take ahead of time when the tempo changes (in a worker), cached per tempo. **Undo / Redo** (↶ ↷ beside Zoom, ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z, Ctrl+Y): `state/EditHistory.svelte.js`, `arranger.history`, covers the clips and the tempo curve (a list of song fields, one line each to add more); each step keeps only the fields it changed, so it never undoes something changed elsewhere; opening another song clears it. The piano roll's own tempo lane isn't in it yet. Not yet: crossfades between clips, copy/paste, more than one picked clip, snapping, takes in the exported mix (the clips are the seam: render them offline). Not tried by ear or on a real iPhone yet. The playhead follows `player.melodyTimeNow` (`tracks/followPlayhead.js`). Desktop first, like Transcribe: on a phone the controls wrap and the grid scrolls sideways, but the 186 px lane headers leave little room. Not yet: bar back/forward, input monitoring, choosing the input, more than four audio lanes. Not tried on a real iPhone yet.
