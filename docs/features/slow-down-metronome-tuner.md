# Slow-down player, metronome and tuner

- **The slow-down player** (`src/lib/slowDowner/`, the stretcher in `$lib/audio/stretch/`; it is the engine under the arranger's Transcribe, the old `/transcribe` page is gone): an Amazing Slow Downer for learning songs by ear (fast bluegrass: lots of notes, sharp pick attacks). Plays the MP3s in `static/audio/` (the list is `songs/songs.js`) at 25–150% with the pitch kept. The page decodes the whole song and hands it to the `stretch-player` worklet (`StretchPlayer.svelte.js`; play/pause fade over 10 ms; Space plays/pauses), which runs `stretch/stretcher.js`: a phase vocoder with phase gradient heap integration (Průša & Holighaus, "Phase vocoder done right"; `phaseGradient.js`). Each bin's exact frequency and where in the frame its energy sits are measured with reassignment windows (`analyseFrame.js`, so any speed works), and both channels get the phase change worked out on their mix (the stereo picture stays). Hann frames, 8× overlap; the frame length is the player's Sound switch (`sounds.js`, plain data: Crisp 23 ms, Balanced 46 ms (the default), Smooth 93 ms; saved in `settings.transcribe.sound`; a change rebuilds the stretcher in the worklet from where it is). Which one the user keeps picking says whether a frame size per frequency range is worth building; at exactly 100% it gives back the source bit for bit. A jump primes the frames so the first sample heard is the jump point at full level. That worklet imports its DSP, so `worklets.js` loads it with `?worker&url` (bundled into one iife file). Checked in Node (scratch script): frequency to 0.001 Hz, click time to the sample, pitch and level kept at half speed, 79% of a click's energy within 2 ms at 50% (a plain phase vocoder: 17%), about 75 ms of CPU per second of output. Checked live in the browser pane (Chrome): plays, speed, jumps, switching songs. Not tried on an iPhone yet. Ideas not built: transient handling beyond PGHI (a click still spreads a little), a frame size per frequency range (short for the highs, long for the bass), an A–B loop on a waveform, key change.
- **The metronome page** (`/metronome`, in the top bar, its picture on a phone; `src/lib/metronome/`): just a click, apart from any song. A really big metronome (`MetronomeIcon`) is the start/stop button (Space too), its pendulum swinging on the beat; big − / + for the tempo; sound and level; a straight click, no time signature or accent (the user's call; `steadyClickGrid`, `SoloClick.svelte.js`, straight to the speakers, no mixer; tempo in `settings.metronome`). It shares the click sounds, grid and clicker with the band's click (`arranger/playback/metronome/`).
- **The tuner** (`src/lib/tuner/`, its DSP in `$lib/audio/tuner/`, the `tuner` worklet; a tuning fork in the top bar next to Metronome, on every page, and one on Transcribe's control bar; opening it pauses whatever is playing (the band, Transcribe's recording, the metronome, the songs list: each signs up with `$lib/audio/players.js`), so the mic hears only you; `TunerButton` drops onto any page). Chosen after research (McLeod & Wyvill; dsego/strobe-tuner; 29a.ch; the One Euro filter): two stages, like the strobe tuners. **Which note**: McLeod's NSDF by FFT on 4096 samples every 1024 (`findPitch.js`), through `noteLock.js` (a new note holds 50 ms with readings agreeing within 8 cents, 120 ms for an octave/fifth/fourth of the shown one; the shown note keeps to 60 cents). **How many cents**: a lock-in (one DFT bin at the note's exact frequency on the sample clock, `lockIn.js`) on the first four partials every 128 samples, a Hann window of 8 whole periods (at least 80 ms, so its nulls sit on the other partials), and a least-squares line through each partial's phase over 0.4 s (`phaseSlope.js`), partials weighed by strength × partial² (a phone mic's weak low E reads from its second partial); restarted on a pluck or when the note stops; McLeod's cents until it has 80 ms. While a note fades the last cents are held. The needle: One Euro in cents (`needleFilter.js`). Neural detectors (CREPE, SwiftF0) were ruled out: no better at sub-cent, and a model to load. Checked in Node (scratch script, synthetic plucks): within 0.3 cent clean, ~1 cent with heavy noise, E1 bass 0.01, a stiff string (B = 2e-4) 0.6 sharp (its upper partials really are), a peg turn followed ~0.25 s behind; 34 ms of CPU a second. Checked in the browser pane with a stubbed mic (oscillators): +3.0 and +0.4 cents read exactly and hold still, phone width, light and dark. Not tried on a real instrument, a phone mic or an iPhone. A = 440 (`TunerState.referenceA`, passed to the worklet; no control for it yet). **Two looks**, a Trace | Meter switch at the top of the dialog (`tunerLooks.js`, a list: a new look is a component taking `tuner` plus one entry; remembered in `settings.tuner.look`): **Trace** (the default, `TunerTrace.svelte`, drawn by `drawTrace.js` on the shared frame loop): the note big with Hz and cents beside it, and the last 4 s of pitch flowing down a canvas, newest at the top, over a grid that flows with it; it casts a drop shadow and glows, with two faint swaying echoes (still with reduced motion). Its colour means how far off you are: green within 2 cents, amber within 10, red beyond (the meter tokens, read from the theme by `themeColour.js`). Sketched with the user first: a perspective road (dropped: they didn't like it tilting away; depth from shadows instead), a silk ribbon and paper strips (kept as sketches only). **Meter** (`TunerMeter.svelte`): the first look, a needle over ±50 cents, flat/sharp lamps, a strobe band and a note wheel. Both screens are `--tuner-screen` (tokens.css), a deeper tone of the page.
