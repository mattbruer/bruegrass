# Piano roll and TAB

- **Piano roll** (the Melody tab's Draw view; its ABC view is the text box, the same tune either way, `TabSwitch` from `$lib/components/`; logic `melody/roll/`, UI `components/pianoRoll/`): draw the melody and its harmony parts on a grid over the chart's bars and chords; each note plays as drawn (`playback/auditionNote.js`, through the Melody channel). **The ABC stays the saved tune**: the roll reads every voice (`readScore.js`, one voice at a time: bars as written, repeats not played out, so a bar is one place to edit; times in ticks, 48 to a quarter, so triplets are exact) and every finished edit rewrites only the changed bars of that voice in place (`scoreToAbc.js`, `writeBar.js`: ties over bar lines and chord changes, triplets where notes fall on thirds of a beat, rests on the beat, spelling by `musicInKey.js` `melodyNote`), keeping the header, repeats and chord symbols (`drawnMelody.js`). No ABC yet: the roll is shaped like the chart (`scoreFromChart.js`) and the first note writes a whole tune with the chart's chords at concert pitch. ABC the app writes from the chart (a blank tune, the band's own bass line, a section's rests) carries the chart's key changes as `[K:]`s at concert pitch (`scoreFromChart` bars get `key`/`keyText`; `newAbc` writes one where a bar's key changes). An inline `[K:]` (or a `K:` line) in the tune is followed bar by bar: each `ScoreBar` keeps the key signature it's under (`key`, none for the tune's own K:), and its bar is written in it, so renaming the song's key respells only the bars under the tune's own K:; harmony parts copy the melody's `[K:]`s and spell in them. Tunes with a key change inside a bar, ornaments, grace notes, slurs or a tie from only part of a chord are shown read-only, with a note to edit them in the ABC view. **Stacks** (double stops, chords; `editNotes.js`): notes that start together in a voice are one stack, written as an ABC chord (`[DF]2`, tied as `[DF]2-`) and sharing one length: a note added to a stack takes its length, resizing any note of it resizes them all; otherwise one note at a time, and a note drawn over others replaces them (full polyphony, notes overlapping with any lengths, was weighed and left: tied chords in the ABC). **A stack is strummed, never struck at once**: a downstroke, low string first, by the guitar's own right hand (`guitar.js` `strumOffsets`, the pendulum, so the faster the tempo the tighter: a double stop about 24 ms at 60 BPM, 10 ms at 200); `melody.js` `strumStacks` for the band (on the strings `finger` picked), `transcription/heardParts.js` for Transcribe (neighbouring middle strings, at the recording's tempo at that beat). A stack's notes graze (see Pluck and graze below). MIDI export and the bass don't strum yet; Transcribe's parts neither strum lighter nor graze. Harmony writing reads a stack's top note. **Zoom** on the roll's toolbar (`settings.pianoRoll.zoom`, one for every roll, the arranger's and Transcribe's, apart from Transcribe's grid; TAB zooms only across, `ZoomControl`'s `axes`). **Octave down / Octave up** on the roll's toolbar (`RollToolbar.svelte`, `melody/roll/octaves.js`) move every note of the part being edited; the melody takes its harmony parts with it (`transposedDrawnMelody`), so hand edits to them stay; refused when a note would leave the piano's keys. Mouse: drag to draw, drag a note (a grab hand) or its right end (a ↔ cursor over the zone that resizes, `resizeEdge` in `noteGestures.js`, kept through the drag), double-click deletes; keys: arrows, Delete. **Touch is its own case** (the user's call, 2026-11-24: on an iPad, dragging a note's end and telling a scroll from a drag were confusing): **a swipe always scrolls, over notes too; only the picked note moves**. Tap an empty spot adds a note (and picks it), tap a note picks it (`pick` gesture: not held, so a swipe from a note still scrolls; only `.note.selected` has `touch-action: none`), then drag it, or drag its **length handle**, a finger-sized tab off its right end (beside the note, so the finger doesn't hide the end it moves; shown on the picked note with a mouse too); **the note bar** under the roll (`NoteEditBar.svelte`, touch screens only, always there so the roll doesn't jump): Move ← →, Pitch ↓ ↑, Length − + by a grid step or semitone, and Delete (`stepEdit` in `noteGestures.js`, which the arrow keys use too); double-tap deletes (two real taps only: a swipe then a tap never counts). Decided per press by `pointerType`, so an iPad with a trackpad gets the mouse's way. Not tried on a real iPad yet; not built: scrolling the roll while a dragged note nears its edge. Drafts come for free (the ABC is a song field).
  - **Generated harmony lines** (the whole story is in [docs/arranger/melody-harmony-bass.md](../arranger/melody-harmony-bass.md), Harmony lines): written from a lead (the melody or a part you wrote) from its lane's ⋯ menu on Tracks or the Draw view's Harmony lines for the melody; each is a lane of its own that follows its lead and the chords a bar at a time. They're edited like any part, and **a bar you change is locked** so it's never written over; the lock at the end of each bar on the ruler (`PianoRoll`'s `locks`, faint when open, the accent when locked) locks or unlocks it, and unlocking writes the bar again at once. "Edit:" picks the voice, each in its own colour (`voice-colour` in `theme/ui.css`, a turn round the colour wheel from the accent: the melody 0°, the bass 180°, each added part its own hue, `LANE_HUES`), with the others faint behind it and the line it's written from outlined. A picked note shows dots on the chord's other notes nearby (`chordToneOptions.js`); tap one to move it there (that locks its bar). Octave down/up moves a lane with the lines generated from it; a generated line doesn't move on its own. Chords under the grid are read from the melody's bars for every voice.
  - **Other ways to sing this line** (`components/pianoRoll/HarmonyOptions.svelte`, shown while a generated line is in the roll): the line in every style of `melody/harmony/harmonyStyles.js` (Bluegrass stack, Smooth, Holds through passing notes, Fewest moves, Parallel thirds and sixths, Passing chords; a new style is one entry), the same results offered once (`melody/roll/harmonyOptions.js`). Tap one to swap it in for the whole line, all but its locked bars (the old "just this bar" scope went: lock a bar instead); the line keeps that style when it follows. While playing, the band restarts from the loop start once the new line is read, so loop a few bars and tap through. **Just the lead and its lines** solos that group; Put back returns the line to before the first try.
  - **The pickup goes round** (`melody/roll/pickupEcho.js`): a tune with a pickup loops, so the pickup is also the last beats of the last bar. The roll (and TAB) show it in both places, the echo outlined dashed, and either can be edited (`MelodyDrawing.echo`; `commit` takes the pickup from whichever copy changed); it's still written once, in the pickup bar. A note drawn in the echo is a pickup note; notes the last bar already had there stay its own. A string chosen in TAB applies to both copies. Not on the bass or lanes (written as played). The sheet music shows it too (`pickupsOnPaper`, not editable there).
  - **Following the band**: while playing, a line on the roll shows where the band is, mapped from the played music to the written bars (`melody/roll/playheadTicks.js`), and the roll scrolls with it, a third of the way across, keeping the sounding note in view (it lets go for 3 s when you scroll yourself).
  - **Starting a tune**: the ABC tab's "Start the tune from the chart" (empty ABC only; `Arranger.startMelodyFromChart`) writes the header and a rest in every bar with the chart's chords, through the same `blankScoreForSong` + `newAbc` the roll's first note uses. A Pickup choice next to it (none to 3 beats) adds a short first bar (`z2 |`) with every later bar's time shifted by it; the roll's first note still starts without one.
  - **The bass line is editable too** (Bass in "Edit:", `bass/`, UI `components/pianoRoll/BassLineNote.svelte`). Until it's edited it's the band's own line, generated as before (`bassline.js`); the roll shows it written out as ABC (`bassAbc.js`). The first edit saves it in the song's own `bass` field (not the tune's ABC, so it never prints under the melody): one ABC voice, bass clef, concert pitch, one bar per chart bar in play order (no repeats), chord symbols on every bar. The roll reads and writes it with the same `readScore`/`rewriteAbc`, opening lower (`BASS_ROWS` in `pitchRows.js`: where each part usually plays; every roll's rows are the piano's 88 keys, `PIANO_ROWS`), with its own bars and chords; the tune isn't drawn behind it. `bass/songBass.js` `bassPart` is the one place that picks edited or generated, for live playback (a loop region plays its slice), export and MIDI; an edited line plays the same every pass, with a fixed gate (`readBass.js`). When the chart changes, `state/bassFollowsChart.svelte.js` rewrites only the bars whose chords or length changed (old and new bars matched in order like a text diff, `bass/matchBars.js`, so adding or removing a bar anywhere keeps the other bars' edits); naming the key respells it (`respellBass.js`). "Rewrite from the chart" empties the field. Edit notes play on the upright (`auditionBassNote`). Notes outside the upright's natural range (`instruments/instrumentRanges.js` `UPRIGHT_RANGE`: its low E up to the G an octave above the open G string, the band's own line's floor too) are striped on the roll, with a line under it saying why (`MelodyDrawing.playable`, `PianoRoll`'s `playable`; another instrument's range is one entry).
  - **TAB is a view of its own** (the Melody tab's Draw | TAB | ABC, and Transcribe's Piano roll | Score | TAB; `MelodyDrawing`'s `view`, UI `pianoRoll/TabView.svelte`, `TabStrip.svelte`, `TabChoice.svelte`; `tab/`; not the bass yet): "TAB for" Guitar, Mandolin or Banjo per part (Guitar until you choose; the choice is saved on the first fret or string change) and a **Tuning** (`tab/tunings.js`, data: each instrument's usual tunings as steps from standard per string, top line first, e.g. Drop D, Open G, DADGAD, cross tunings, Double C, Sawmill; Custom… shows a note picker per string, −7 to +4 semitones; `tunedBoard` moves the strings' open pitches and names them; saved as `tuning`, the steps, in the part's TAB, none for standard; retuning keeps your string choices and the frets follow (the TAB's `{#each}` blocks are keyed by what each row shows; after a hot reload of a `.svelte.js` state module an open page can hold two copies of its state, so reload before judging a bug like "the menu changes but nothing follows"); `TuningChoice.svelte`), the chords above it over their first notes, a line per string, top line first, bar lines, each note's fret number centred on its string's line where it starts, a little in from the barline as TAB is written (`tab/tabLayout.js` `tabX`: bars keep the roll's width, notes spread over what's left after an 18 px lead-in; the playhead and seeking use the same map), the bars numbered under it (click to play from there in Transcribe), at the roll's scale and zoom across (`melody/roll/rollLayout.js`, shared with the roll), following the band. It sat under the piano roll at first; the user moved it to its own view. Instruments are data (`tab/fretboards.js`; pitches as they sound, concert like the piano roll, for every instrument (the user's call, Nov 2026: what the roll says is what plays; guitar and banjo were once an octave up, as their music is written); the banjo's 5th string starts at fret 5). Each note goes on the string you chose, else lowest on the neck (a double stop: each its own string, lowest highest fret, `tab/fingering.js`). Tap a number to pick its note (the roll's own selection, kept when switching views on Transcribe); then type a fret to change the note's pitch on that string (two digits within 0.8 s make 10 and up; `pitchAt`), ↑ ↓ for the string above or below at the same pitch (`stringToward`), or Delete. Under the TAB, while a TAB note is picked, `TabFretBar.svelte`: a Fret field (a phone's number pad) and ↑ String / ↓ String buttons, for touch. A fret the string hasn't is ignored. The pitch change goes through the roll's `changeNote`. Your choices are saved with the song (`tab` field: part → `{ instrument, strings: { "start:midi": string }, tuning? }`, `tab/songTab.svelte.js`); choosing another instrument starts them afresh, and choices for notes that moved are dropped on the next choice. The user chose this layout (draw in the roll, fix the fingering in TAB) over typing frets on the lines, and may switch: typing would be a new editing view over the same `fretboards.js` and `fingering.js`. Not built: typing on an empty spot of the TAB to add a note, the capo, TAB in the sheet music (abcjs draws `tablature` for guitar, mandolin and `fiveString`, but picks its own strings), bass TAB.
