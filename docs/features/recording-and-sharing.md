# Recording, takes and sharing

- **Recording along** (Record on the Tracks page, on the armed audio lane, `components/tracks/`, `components/recording/`; logic `takes/`, state `state/RecordingState.svelte.js`, saved by `storage/songTakes.js`): record a voice or a real instrument along with the band, on one of four record tracks (`takes/recordTracks.js`, each its own mixer channel, shown only while it has a take; on the Tracks page each is an audio lane, added with + Audio lane; replace the band's guitar by muting it and recording on a track). Record always gives a count-in (1 or 2 bars, `settings.metronome.countInBars`, the same as Play's, chosen beside Record on the Tracks page; played by the player, below), then the band plays the song (or the loop region) once and stops by itself. With a melody pickup, the count-in clicks carry on under it; Stop or Play/Stop ends it early. Built for a co-writing workflow with no backend yet: a friend records a voice on his own device, taps Download, sends the WAV, and you add it with "Add a take from a file".
  - **One clock**: the mic and recorder (`take-recorder` worklet, which can now start on an exact audio-clock frame) open in Tone's own context, so a take and the band share a sample clock. The take is moved earlier by the round trip the browser reports (`outputLatency` + the mic track's `latency`, `recordAlong.js`), kept as `latencyMs`; Earlier/Later nudges it by ear (`nudgeMs`). Measured calibration is still to come.
  - **A take sits on the song's timeline**: `startsAt` in seconds from bar 1, at the tempo it was recorded at, and **follows the tempo**: at another tempo it plays stretched, its pitch kept (`takes/takeTempo.js`; see player-and-tracks.md). What plays is the lane's clips, each a window onto a take (`clips.js`; the take in use whole until you edit the lane on the Tracks page, see player-and-tracks.md). Each pass plays the part of each clip that falls in it (`takeTiming.js` `sliceForPass`; before bar 1 only from the top, like the melody's pickup). Takes play as one more scheduler stream (`playTakes.js`). The band waits only for the tune's written pickup or a count-in, and a take's sound before bar 1 plays within that wait. Nothing is guessed from a take's audio: guessing a sung pickup from it (`takePickup.js`) was tried and taken off, Nov 2026, because count-in bleed made the band wait a whole bar on every Play (the user's call: the song's pickup is the tune's). Recording leaves its own track out.
  - **Take files start one bar before bar 1** (the count-in bar, where a pickup is sung), whatever the melody does, so a file lines up in the same song on any device and in a DAW.
  - Takes are their own documents in IndexedDB (not in the song document), saved the moment they're recorded; they belong to a song (recording on a template makes it your own song first). Older takes on a track are kept; one is in use. When a backend comes, `songTakes.js` is the module that syncs.
  - No input monitoring: you hear yourself with one ear off, or through an interface's direct monitoring. Bluetooth headphones are warned about by input name. iPhone and iPad: `navigator.audioSession` is set in one place, `$lib/audio/audioSession.js`: every mic (`openMicInput`: recording, the tuner, the sampler) holds it at `play-and-record` while open (without it the mic refuses: "AudioSession category is not compatible with audio capture"), and playing asks for `playback` only when no mic is open. Not tried on a real iPhone yet.
  - Not yet: takes in the exported mix and stems (their track channels are left out of stems), sending the song itself to a collaborator, choosing the input, input monitoring.
- **A/V lanes** film as they record: see [video.md](video.md).
- **Sending a song to another device** (Export → Share with a bandmate; logic `share/`, UI `components/share/`): no backend, so the song travels by hand. One plain package (`share/songPackage.js`, versioned): the song document as on the page (unsaved changes included), the sender's own chord shapes it uses (My chords), and every take (which is in use on each track rides along). Two ways: a **link** (no takes; the package deflated into the URL after `#song=`, which never reaches a server; the starter song with its mix is about 1.3k characters) or a **song file** (`.bluegrass`, a zip: `song.json` plus each take's audio). On a phone the share sheet (prepared as soon as the way is picked: iOS only opens it straight from a tap); on a computer Copy link / Save file (`handOver.js`). Receiving: opening a link (or pasting one: `hashchange`), Open → "Open a song file…", or dropping a file on the page on a computer (`share/dropSongFile.js`) shows `ReceiveSongDialog`; opening saves it among your songs at once and opens it. Same song id already here: Update my copy (fields replaced, your own takes kept, received takes updated in place) or Keep both. Nothing received deletes anything; a chord name you already have keeps your shape (the notes say so).
  - **Takes are recorded at full quality and compressed only to send** (`share/takeFormat.js`, the one place: `SEND_FORMAT`; reading follows whatever format the file names, so a new format is one entry in `FORMATS`). Now MP3 at 128 kbps per channel (`share/mp3.js`, about 1 MB a minute): the one compressed format every browser both plays and (with the LAME encoder in plain JS, `@breezystack/lamejs`, LGPL, lazily loaded, only imported there) can make. A received take is `quality: 'compressed'` and keeps the file it came in (`sent` beside its audio in IndexedDB), so sharing the song again passes it on byte for byte, never compressed twice. Paid plans are meant to send lossless (WAV) here; free stays compressed.
  - **MP3's leading silence**: decoders differ (measured: Chrome and ffmpeg 1105 samples, Safari 26 on iOS 576), so `leadingSilence` measures this browser once (a click through encoder and decoder) and every received take is cut to start there and to its exact length. Checked: Chrome, ffmpeg and iOS Safari (simulator) put the clicks on the exact sample at 44.1 and 48 kHz.
  - Checked in the iOS Simulator: Share file opens the share sheet, Save to Files works, the `.bluegrass` file isn't greyed out in the picker, and Open it saves and opens the song. The simulator's screenshots lag a step behind taps: tap once, then look twice. Not tried on a real iPhone.
