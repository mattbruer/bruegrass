# Arrangement text, timing and guitar

Part of the arranger notes; the short rules are in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md).

## Arranger architecture

### Arrangement text

Parsed by `parseArrangement.js`. Example: `"Chorus"|G,G|C,C|G,G|D,D|`, plus `"Break" as Chorus` for repeats.

- Each line is a section.
- `|` separates measures and `,` separates chords within a measure. A bar's beats are split evenly between its chords, with any odd beat going to the earlier chords. An empty bar (`|G| |`, or `||`) keeps the chord before it, as in written music (not a stop: that's N.C.); `chart/chartTokens.js` is the one rule for what counts as a bar, shared by the parser and the writers that find a bar in the text (`withBarChords`, `analysisMarks`). The parser keeps which bars were empty (`held`, parallel to `measures`, carried through `as` and loop regions); `chartBars.js` marks them (never the first bar) and every view draws them without a chord name: the strip, the help examples, Transcribe's chord lane (an empty chip; `barChords.js` `isHeld`: slots with no chord, while an N.C. bar has none stored; opening and leaving one keeps it held, clearing a written bar makes it N.C.; `chartFromBars` writes it back empty), and the Tracks chord blocks (one block stretched over it).
- `N.C.` (no chord) is a stop: the band rests there (`|G,N.C.|` for half a bar). It names no key. `chart/noChord.js` is the one place the band learns it: `sectionsToPlay` fills each N.C. with the chord before so instruments can work out their parts, then `withoutStops` drops notes starting in one and `endBeforeStops` cuts ringing guitar notes (used by `instruments/performance.js`, `export/midi.js`, and `bass/songBass.js` `bandsBassLine` for the band's own bass; an edited bass plays as written). `guessedKey` skips it; harmony and the roll's chord dots get no chord there; a blank tune writes no chord symbol for it.
- `[K:G]` names the key from there on, carrying into later sections like `[M:]` (a B part in minor, a modulation); inside a bar it starts at the next chord, for a pivot chord (`|Em,[K:G]D7|`). The parser keeps each chord's key as `keys`, parallel to `measures` (null before any is named; `chart/key.js` `keyOfChord`), carried through `as`, loop regions (`loop.js`), slots (`SlotPosition.key`), chart bars and the roll's chords under the bars. Only for analysis and spelling, never what's played: Roman (`analyzeProgression` takes a key per chord and reads the chords around it in that chord's key, so a pivot V7 is V7 of the new key), Nashville/shapes/concert labels, the chord tutor (`ChordAnalysis.key`), concert chord names (`concertChordAt`, `abcWithChords`, `scoreFromChart`) and transposing (`transposeChart`: every `[K:]` moves with its own key). One before the first bar names the song's key ("The song's key" above).
- **A chord the band can't play is pointed out as it's typed** (`chart/knownChords.js`, the one check: N.C. or a name the chord book has a shape for, built in or built from its notes): the chart editor's red line names it and its bar, Play says the same (`arrangement.error`; `readError` is only an unreadable chart, which is what stops Transcribe writing), Transcribe refuses it in the chord boxes (the bar stays open, the box framed, the message under it) and draws a chart's unknown chord as a red chip.
- **Hits** (`G~` rings, `G!` is choked after a beat; `chart/hits.js`, the one place the marks are read): the band strikes the chord once and stops strumming until the next chord written in the chart (empty bars after it ring on), for an ending or a stop mid-song. Where it lands is the comma rule: `|G~|` on the downbeat, `|D7,G~|` on beat 3. The parser keeps it as `hits`, parallel to `measures` (and on each `SlotPosition` as `hit`), carried through `as` and loop regions; transposing, Transcribe and the chord check keep or strip it. The guitar plays the strum patterns `ring` (`X...`) and `choke` (`X.m.`): `X` strums all six strings, `m` mutes what's ringing (`MUTE_FADE`); the bass plays the root, held, choked where the guitar's pattern mutes (`songBass.js` `withHits`, from `mutedAfter`); the mandolin chops once on a choked hit and lays out on a ringing one (a chop can't ring: `CHOPPED_HITS` in `mandolin.js`). The chord strip doesn't show the mark yet. A strum editor would write per-bar patterns in the same characters.
- **A bar holds one chord, or one on each of its chord places** (`chart/chordPlaces.js`, the one rule: three in 3/4, two in every other bar, the user's call): `|G,D|` is half each in 4/4, but an error in 3/4, where `|G,G,D|` holds G for two beats. `chordCountProblem` is checked with unknown chords (`arrangement.error`), so the editor's red line names the bar and Play waits.
- `A7{V7/V}`: your own analysis of a chord, for the Roman display; the band ignores it (`chart/analysisMarks.js`). A hit goes before it: `G~{I}`.
- Time signatures use abc-style inline fields:
  - `[M:3/4]` lasts until changed, carrying into later sections.
  - `[2/4]` applies to one bar only (crooked tunes).
  - The default is 4/4.
  - Only denominators 4 and 2 are supported.
- Sections store `measures: string[][]` plus a parallel `meters`.
- **When the syntax changes, update the help page** `src/routes/arranger/help/+page.svelte`.
  - Its audience is non-technical, so keep it in plain language with one idea per section.
  - Each example goes through `{@render example(...)}`, which draws it as a chart. The examples must parse.

### Two ways to edit the chart

**Set aside for now** (the user's call: it needs work): `OFFER_BUTTONS = false` in `ArrangementEditor` hides the switch and always shows Text; set it to true to bring Buttons back. Everything below is built and committed.

The chart editor (`ArrangementEditor`, so the editor drawer and Transcribe both have it) switches between **Text** (typing, with the keypad on touch) and **Buttons** (`components/builder/`), remembered on this device (`settings.chartEditor`). The text stays the one place the song lives: Buttons reads it as written (`chart/writtenChart.js`: sections, bars with their chords and fields, repeats, lyric lines; nothing worked out, so writing back keeps everything typed and only tidies spacing) through `ArrangementState.written`, and each button is a plain function on that (`builder/chartEdits.js`) written back by `editWritten`.

- Parts are cards with their name in a field (typed) and a ⋯ menu: move, copy, play another part again (`as`), give a repeat its own bars, delete (its repeats get its bars written out). Renaming a part renames its repeats.
- Bars are tiles (4 across on a phone, 8 on a wider screen), lit while heard like the text. Tap one, then a chord: the next bar is picked, so a progression is a row of taps; the dashed + tile adds a bar. + chord splits a bar, as far as `chart/chordPlaces.js` allows (the one rule, shared with Transcribe, the strip, typed charts and the tune's chords: up to three chords in 3/4, one a beat, and two in every other bar, on the downbeat and halfway); each chord gets an equal part of its tile, as it gets an equal part of the bar. The chord buttons are the key's own (`builder/chordChoices.js`, plain data: I IV V V7 vi ii iii II7 bVII in major), N.C., and any root and kind (More…).
- The Bar tab adds, copies, holds (empty bar) or deletes a bar and sets its time, for this bar (`[2/4]`) or from here on (`[M:3/4]`) (`builder/barTimes.js`). The Words tab types a bar's words (Enter goes to the next bar), a part's pickup and where a line of words starts; words move with their bar.
- Undo (50 steps) is kept while switching to Text and back; typing in the text clears it. Words typed in one bar undo as one.
- Not in Buttons (still in Text): keys mid-song (`[K:]`, kept as written), slash chords, analysis marks (kept; set from the chord tutor).

### `timeline.js`

The single source of musical time. All times are in quarter notes.

- `slotPositions` gives each chord's start and length.
- `rhythm` gives the boom/chuck grid: `beatGroups` makes 2 = boom-chuck and 3 = boom-chuck-chuck.
- The guitar, mandolin, bass line, MIDI, chart and playhead all derive from it. Never assume a fixed bar length.

### `guitar.js`

The core of the arranger.

- **Chord shapes** come from `guitar-chords.json`. Each shape is 6 frets from low E to high e, with `null` for a muted string. The capo adds to every fret.
- **The chord book** (`instruments/chordBook.js`) is the one place a chord name becomes a shape: `chordShape`/`hasShape`, used by `guitar.js`, the capo choice in `abcChords.js` and the help page. It looks in your own shapes first, then `guitar-chords.json`. Voicings built from theory should plug in here.
- **My chords was removed** (Nov 2026, the user's call): guitar patterns replaced it (`docs/features/player-and-tracks.md`, Chords on Tracks). A shape of your own is now a change to how the guitar plays one chord of a song (`song.guitarChords`), copied, pasted or saved as a pattern for every song. The chord book plays the built-in shapes, then one built from the chord's notes, so `Cadd9` still plays. A variant name (`G:high`) is still read: the part before the colon says what the chord is (`withoutVariant`), and it plays the plain chord's shape. Old shapes stay in this browser (`bruegrass.chordVoicings`), listed on the storage page as no longer used.
- `guitar-chords.json` has major, minor, 7 and dim in every key (both sharp and flat names), lowest position, open strings where they fit. **Slash chords** (`G/B`) strum the chord's shape and boom the slash note, the lowest one on the bottom three strings up to fret 4; the alternating boom is the chord's fifth, or its root when the fifth is already the bass (`alternatingPitchClass`). The MIDI bass follows the same rule. The mandolin never plays the slash note (the user's call): its chops are the plain chord (`mandolin.js` reads only the name before the slash). abcjs writes chord flats as ♭; `plainChordName` in `abcChords.js` turns them back.
- **Samples** are in `static/samples/guitar/{E,A,D,G,B,ee}{0..12}.mp3`.
- **`buildEvents`** turns sections into timed note events:
  - Each beat group of the rhythm grid gets a **strum pattern** (`strumPatterns.js`, plain data): one character per stroke of a hand that keeps moving down and up (even = down, odd = up), `B` bass, `x` strum, `.` miss, at `steps` strokes per quarter. Boom-chuck and bum-titty (`B.xx`: a quarter, then two eighths down-up; in 3/4 `B.x.xx`) mixed at random, half and half for now (`STRUM_MIX`, seeded so export and MIDI match live's first pass). A new pattern is one entry.
  - Root and fifth alternate on the `B`s within a run of the same chord; both are found as the lowest note in the shape with the right pitch class.
  - A strum is strings 3–5; an upstroke plays them high to low, 3 dB lighter. A light upstroke graze (top two strings) sounded wrong with these samples: it cut the chuck's ring short and replaced it quieter. Try again with real graze samples.
  - A string sounds one note at a time: striking it again damps the old note, which fades in 15 ms from the moment the new one starts (`RESTRIKE_FADE`). A chord change fades it in 50 ms (`CHORD_CHANGE_FADE`). Looping, the guitar's notes span 4 passes (`LOOP_PASSES`), so the sprinkle varies.
- **Strum spread** uses a pendulum model of the right hand (string spacing and swing amplitude vs. tempo; faster strokes swing smaller). Hypothesis to test: bluegrass is more a bounce (the hand speeds up and slows down through the stroke) than a pendulum; Irish keeps the pendulum.
- Tried and dropped (too subtle to hear, or not wanted): a fake round robin (nudging each sample's pitch, level and start; real round robins come with the recorded samples) and a hand feel (one random force per stroke setting level, brightness, sweep speed and timing).
- **Pluck and graze** (`instruments/articulation.js`, plain data, the user's ear): a single note is plucked (the pick grabs the string), every strummed note grazes (it brushes past), whatever the tempo; a strum never has a pluck's attack. Booms pluck, the guitar's strums graze, and so do the tune's stacks. The mandolin's chops have their own articulation, `chop` (`mandolin.js` `CHOP_ARTICULATION`): no skip, no fade-in, a 9 kHz low-pass on the channel (`band.js`), so the pick's attack survives. On top, `instruments/chopSound.js` adds the percussion the samples lack: a 30 ms noise "tk" per chop (on its first course; band-passed around 2.2 kHz, at −2 dB), a low-pass per chop sweeping 6 kHz → 1.4 kHz over 20 ms, and a fade-out so the chop stops at 80 ms (tuned toward percussion, Nov 2026: the user heard the chuck buried under the notes; it was a 15 ms tk at −10 dB, 8 → 2 kHz, 120 ms). A recorded chuck on muted strings is planned; it replaces the tk in `createChopTick`. All tuning constants are there. Faked from the plucked samples until graze samples are recorded (then a graze picks those, in that one place): 4 dB quieter, a 5 kHz low-pass, the pick's click skipped (the sample starts 6 ms past where its sound begins, `soundStart.js`, measured once per sample in the browser: Chrome decodes about 11 ms of MP3 silence before the pick, ffmpeg 23 ms; the note still lands on time) and a 4 ms fade-in. The user likes the guitar's; the mandolin's chops still sound off, probably the samples. The boom and strum faders on the guitar's strip were removed once the graze did their job: the balance between boom and strum is the articulation's, not the mix's, plus the rhythm guitar's own `STRUM_DB` (−6 dB in `guitar.js`, so its strums sit 10 dB under the booms: the user found them too loud; the tune's strummed stacks still graze at −4) (MIDI velocities follow the articulation). Ideas not built: shaping only the first 10–20 ms (transient), a thinner body (a little low-mid cut).
- **Samples in tune** (`instruments/sampleTuning.js`): every guitar sample was measured with the tuner (`node scripts/measureSampleTuning.js`, over each note's first 1.2 s, since quiet tails drift; rerun when samples change) and its fix in cents is kept in `instruments/guitarSampleFixes.js`, one line per sample named like its file, most in tune first. `band.js` multiplies the fix into each sample's playback rate (strums, booms, the tune on guitar; `auditionNote` too), always on (an A/B switch in Settings was tried; the user couldn't hear a difference, but it's kept: free, and long notes and new samples benefit). Before: average 3 cents off, worst 15 (the low E string runs sharp); after, re-measured: within 0.4. The mandolin chops are too short and choked to measure, so they're left as recorded.
- **Every sample is struck on the beat** (`instruments/soundStart.js` `startOnTheBeat`, used by `band.js` and, for a tune instrument marked `struck` (the band's guitar), `tuneVoice.js`): the strike is where the sound first reaches 20% of the sample's peak, measured as each sample loads, so the MP3's leading silence and a recording's quiet pick scrape before the note (ee8 had about 35 ms of it, A5 22, ee7 and B5 12) are passed over; a pluck keeps 5 ms before the strike, a graze starts past it. Before this, the 5% point counted as the start, so those samples sounded late (uneven eighths in On and On's pickup), and every plucked note landed about 23 ms behind the tune's synths and the click. The tune played on the band's guitar missed this at first and started every sample from its first byte, so a note on ee8 (strike at 62 ms against 24–38 for its neighbours) dragged.
- **The tempo lane** (above the arranger's piano roll, through `PianoRoll`'s `lane` seam, and under the Tracks page's ruler, where Undo covers it: `components/tempo/TempoLane.svelte`, the host saying where a place is across, `xAt`, and the place under a pointer, snapped, `quartersAt`; not on Transcribe, where the recording sets the tempo): draw tempo changes like Logic's tempo track, a ritard at the end say. Bar 1 is always at the song's tempo; points bend it, in straight lines from one to the next, holding after the last; a new point lands on the line where you click, so it changes nothing until dragged (a click near the song's tempo snaps to it). Click/tap adds, drag moves (snapped to the roll's grid, never past a neighbour), double-click/tap deletes, ↑ ↓ a BPM, Delete. Points are `{ at, share }`: quarters from bar 1 as played, times the song's tempo (so the Tempo control still scales the whole song), saved as `tempoCurve`, `ArrangementState.tempoCurve`. `chart/tempoCurve.js` `tempoClock` is the one place quarters become seconds with the curve (exact per stretch, a logarithm, so loops never drift); `preparePerformance` bends every note's time with it after swing, so live, audio export, the click (`clicker.js` takes the clock), the playhead (`beats()`/`position()`) and takes' region start (`ArrangementState.clock`) all follow. A loop region gets the curve from where it starts. A change restarts a playing band. Not yet: the MIDI file's tempo track, points following bars when the chart's bars change (they stay on their quarter), a tune with repeats shows a point only where its music is first played.
- **Loop on/off** (the Loop button that starts the loop bar, `LoopBar.svelte`; `LoopState.repeats`, kept as this device's habit in `settings.loopPlayback`, not with the song): off, Play goes through the song (or the chosen bars) once and stops, for an ending or a ritard. `PlayerState.play` is the one place a playback that doesn't loop is stopped, once its last pass has rung out (`RING_OUT`, via `secondsLeft()`); recording uses the same (`passes: 1`). Past the last pass the playhead is gone (`beats()`/`position()` null). Next: loop recording, each pass kept as a take to comp.
- **Stop carries on, with Loop off** (`PlayerState.resumeAt`, quarters from bar 1 of the song): Stop remembers where the band was, the next Play starts there (`playArrangement`'s `from`: bar 1 is put in the past and the notes before now skipped; no pickup or count-in lead; a take that started earlier is skipped whole), and the chord strip rests there while stopped (`restAt`). Back to the start is a key on the player bar (`ToStartTile.svelte`, `ToStartIcon.svelte`), shown only while Loop is off (with it on, Play always starts at the top), dimmed when there's nowhere to go back to; a playing band starts over. Playing to the end, opening a song, or a resume point outside the chosen bars starts at the top. Recording always starts at the top.
- **Swing** (`chart/swing.js`, the Swing fader in the Tempo panel, saved with the song): each beat is bent so its second eighth lands at `swing`% of it, applied once to every note's time in `preparePerformance` (live and audio export). Not yet: the MIDI file, the strum spread (bent with the beat, so an upstroke's strum tightens a little), the click.
- **Lighter touch** above 120 BPM, on top of the articulation: lower level, low-pass filter and softer attack (`touch` in `guitar.js`).
- **`playArrangement`** (now in `playback/livePlayback.js`) places every note at an absolute audio-clock time (`start + pass × duration + offset`).
  - A Tone context timer queues passes within a lookahead, catches up if it's late, and skips past-due notes.
  - Don't use `setTimeout` for musical timing.
  - It returns `{ position(), stop() }`.
