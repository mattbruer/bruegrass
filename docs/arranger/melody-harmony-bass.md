# Melody, harmony, sheet music and the bass

**Drawing is only the sheet music's job** (`components/melody/SheetMusic.svelte`, `melody/drawSheetMusic.js`): abcjs can only draw from text, so `renderAbc` parses again there, and only while the sheet music is on screen. It gives back just the drawn notes' positions for the cursor. A pickup is drawn ending with a double bar line, and its notes are drawn again in the last bar's closing beats where that bar only rests (the tune goes round) (`pickupsOnPaper` in `melody/roll/pickupBar.js`, in `SheetMusic.svelte`: only on the page, the saved ABC keeps its plain bar line and rests). Measured in the browser: one `parseOnly` per keystroke in the ABC box (about 1 ms on Amazing Grace), per roll edit, per harmony option tried; a chart change parses only the band's fresh bass line when the bass is edited. Still parsing for themselves, on purpose: `transposeAbc` (a capo move; abcjs's transposer wants its own parse) and one melody edit in the roll, which parses each in-between version once (words, then harmony).

### `melody.js`

- An optional melody in ABC notation (the "Melody" box on `/arranger`), drawn with abcjs and played through the `melody` mixer channel. abcjs's own playback is not used.
- **What the tune plays on** is picked per tune channel (Melody and every harmony part): a button on its strip (`components/mixer/instruments/InstrumentButton.svelte`) opens one shared modal, `InstrumentWindow.svelte` (`instrumentWindowState.svelte.js` says which channel; mounted once in the band layout, so Transcribe's part lanes open it too, from the same button under their volume), listing every instrument by family; tapping one picks it and, while stopped, plays a note on it (`auditionNote` takes a channel; piano-roll notes audition on the edited voice's). The channel's `instrument` saves with the mix; the defaults give each part its own (`TUNE_INSTRUMENT_DEFAULTS` in `tuneInstruments.js`: melody piano, tenor violin, high baritone flute, baritone clarinet, low tenor cello); an id not in the list plays the channel's default (`channelInstrument`). **Every tune instrument is recorded samples** (the user's call: the synth recipes and the guitar for the tune were dropped): the 22 the user chose by ear from free sample sets (a listening test page, since removed), as data in `instruments/tuneInstruments.js` (`SAMPLE_SETS`: tonejs-instruments, CC BY 3.0, credit nbrosowsky; FluidR3 GM, MIT, every 3rd semitone loaded; `octave` per instrument; level-matched as they load: `instruments/sampleLoudness.js` measures each set's notes and gains them to `TUNE_NOTE_LOUDNESS_DB`, about a single guitar note, since FluidR3's are recorded 15–20 dB below tonejs'; `volumeDb`, all 0, is a by-ear trim on top); a new instrument is one entry. `instruments/tuneVoice.js` plays them: each note the nearest sample, pitched by playback rate, a repeated pitch damps the last; samples load once per page, and `createBand` waits for each tune channel's before the first note (a mid-play change of instrument skips notes until its samples arrive). The samples load from where the sets are published (GitHub Pages): copying them to our own storage is a change to `url` only. `band.js` asks for each note's instrument as it plays it, so a change is heard while playing; export reads the mix's. MIDI export is unchanged. The bass keeps the upright synth.
- `melodyFromTune` takes a parsed abcjs tune. It uses `tune.setUpAudio` (repeats and endings played out) to get notes in quarter notes, timed from bar 1's downbeat, so pickup notes are negative. It finds the pickup itself, because abcjs's `getPickupLength` misses a pickup written after `|:`.
- Sync contract: the melody and the arrangement are written separately and only share a clock. The melody's bar 1 starts with the arrangement's bar 1, at the arranger's tempo (the ABC's `Q:` is ignored). The melody loops with the arrangement's pass length. Anything past the end is cut, and a short melody leaves a gap.
- The pickup plays before the band (`leadIn` delays the band's start) and again at the end of every pass.
- A loop region plays the matching melody bars (`loopOffset` in `loop.js`).
- Pitch: ABC is concert pitch. The tune's notes are fingered as the guitar would play them, at written MIDI −12 (guitar sounds an octave below written) + transpose (the Key setting), and each tune instrument adds its `octave` back: 12 for most, the band's guitar included, so what the piano roll says is what plays (the user's call, Nov 2026). The capo doesn't move it: the arrangement's shapes + capo reach the tune's concert key.
- Moving the capo asks whenever the chart has chords, melody or not (`components/player/CapoChoiceDialog.svelte`, logic in `Arranger.setCapo`): raise the song (the ABC, if any, is rewritten by `melody/transposeAbc.js`, abcjs `strTranspose`) or keep its key (the chart's chords move to new shapes, `chart/transposeChart.js`). Either way the ABC stays at concert pitch, so nothing in the saved shape changed.
- The sheet music has two views once there's a capo or key change (`SheetViewToggle.svelte`): Concert (as it sounds, the Key menu included) and Guitar shapes (the chart's key, to read with the capo on). Display only, via abcjs `visualTranspose`; that also moves chord symbols, so `drawSheetMusic` reads the chord sequence from an untransposed parse. `finger()` picks strings with a position-playing hand model. Notes above the high-e 12th fret sample are resampled.

### `abcChords.js`

- Converts chords both ways between the ABC melody and the arrangement. Both directions read `abcjs.synth.sequence(tune)`, which lists the tune in play order with repeats and endings played out. Each note or rest has `timing` (its place in the written score, in whole notes), `duration`, `chord`, and `elem.startChar`.
- `arrangementFromAbc`:
  - Bars come from the bar lines. A pickup is dropped. A short bar at the end of a repeat is merged with the pickup that follows it.
  - Chords follow the chart's rule (`chart/chordPlaces.js`): a change moves to the nearest place a chord may change on (beats 1 and 3, every beat in 3/4; halfway, it moves later so the bar keeps its first chord), and the bar is written as one chord or one per place (`chordsOnPlaces`, written by `transcription/barChords.js` `chartCell`). A change that loses its place to a later one is left out, and the note says so.
  - Sections split where the score jumps back (a repeat). They're lettered A, B… by where they start in the score, and an identical repeat becomes `"A" as A`.
  - The capo is chosen so chords land on shapes in `guitar-chords.json`, preferring G/C/D/Am/Em home shapes.
- `abcWithChords` (placement in `chordSymbolPlaces`): writes the arrangement's concert-pitch chords into ABC that has none, on the note where each change lands. It reports changes during held notes, and repeated bars the arrangement chords differently on another pass.
- **The chart is the one place chords are kept; the tune's chord symbols are a view of it, both ways** (the user's call: an edit to either is an edit to both). **Chart → tune** (`melody/chordsFollowChart.js` `tuneWithChartChords`, run from `state/tuneFollowsChart.svelte.js` after bars and renames are followed, `Arranger.followChords`): whenever the chart's chords change (compared at concert pitch), every symbol in the tune is written fresh, not only the edited bars, since whether a bar needs a symbol depends on the bar before (G/B in bar 2 then back to G once left bar 3's G off). Annotations (`"^n.c."`) and decorations stay. A tune with no chord symbols is left without. It runs whenever the chart can be read, an unknown chord included. A capo move is skipped (`setCapo` transposes the tune itself). A change that lands inside a held note splits it there and ties the parts (`"C"c4` → `"C"c2-"C/E"c2`, a rest without a tie; `melody/splitHeldNotes.js`, the user's call), so every chord has a note to sit on; the chart's words are then placed again (`words/wordsAgain.js`, shared with the piano roll), a tied note holding its syllable (`_`). A note stays whole when its written length isn't what it plays (a tuplet, broken rhythm: its unit length isn't the tune's usual one), or the tune has words typed only in the ABC (they'd slip a note), and then the change isn't shown, as before. A split stays when the chord is later taken away (it sounds the same). Respelling the key changes every bar's names. A tune's own richer chord (Cmaj7 where the chart says C) isn't kept: put it in the chart. **Tune → chart** (`melody/chartFollowsTune.js`, from `state/ChartFollowsTuneState.svelte.js`, the ABC box in `MelodyPanel.svelte`): after a pause in typing (`PAUSE_MS`, so a half-typed `"Am` never reaches the chart), the bars whose chords the typing changed (read on the chart's bars in play order, a symbol carrying on until the next, as ABC reads) are written into the chart (`withBarChords`, at the guitar's shapes with the capo); only those, so the chart keeps changes the tune can't show. A bar with more changes than places isn't written; the box says which. The chart's change then rewrites the tune's symbols, so a chord typed on beat 2 moves to beat 3. Repeats: the tune's repeats are played out, so a chord edited in a `|: :|` bar is written to every pass; where the chart plays that bar differently on another pass, the tune shows the first pass's chord. Not yet: the piano roll and harmony writes don't read chords back (they don't change chord symbols), and a song opened with chords out of step isn't put right until its chart's chords change.
- Chord shapes are bluegrass voicings with a low fifth, so `bassNote` can alternate root and fifth. Keep that when adding shapes.

### Harmony parts (`melody/harmony/`)

- **Lines from the melody** (`components/melody/HarmonyParts.svelte`): a layout in one tap (`HARMONY_LAYOUTS`: 2 parts, 3 parts, Melody on top) or each line on its own; the melody is shown as **Melody (given)** in the piano roll's voice picker, and stays in view (outlined) behind a line being edited. The **Roots below** line (`singsRoots`, `harmonyStyles.js` `ROOTS`) sings the band's chord root in a bass singer's octave, whatever style the others use.
- Up to four parts (`harmonyParts.js`, data: shown as **Above, Above 2, Below, Below 2**, by where the line sits (the user's call); their ids and sheet-music labels are still the singers' tenor, high baritone, baritone, low tenor, since the label is how a voice finds its mixer channel; the extra parts of + Lane, `melody/extraParts.js`, are voices too, and `partChannels.js` maps both), each counting chord tones from the lead (+1 = next chord tone up). The toggles are `components/melody/HarmonyParts.svelte`; `Arranger.writeHarmonies` writes them.
- `harmonyNote.js`: on a chord tone (root/3rd/5th of the band's concert chord, `chart/concertChords.js`), count along the triad; on any other note, along a close triad built on the melody note from the chord's scale (`theory/chordScales.js`), so parts move in parallel. Sixths come out of this naturally (high baritone over the 3rd).
- `writeHarmonies.js`: each part is the melody's own ABC text with pitches swapped (rhythm, ties, repeats kept; chord symbols, grace notes and lyrics dropped), appended as `V:<id> name="…"` after the melody, which gets a `V:1` line (abcjs plays voices together only then). Rewriting replaces the parts; removing all of them gives back the original ABC. Spelling and accidentals through `abcPitch.js` (`musicInKey.js`). Single-voice melodies only. Repeated bars take the first pass's chord.
- **The parts follow the chart's chords, if you say so** (the user's call: a question first, whole parts rewritten, since rewriting one bar made awkward lines): after a chart edit that changes the chords, `state/HarmonyFollowState.svelte.js` waits for a pause (`PAUSE_MS`, so typing a chord in the chart asks once), then, if the new chords would change any part, asks (`components/melody/HarmonyFollowDialog.svelte`, mounted in the band layout): **Rewrite the harmony** or **Keep it as it is** (closing it keeps them). Rewrite writes each of those parts whole, fresh for the chords now, in its own style (`melody/harmonyFollowsChords.js`): a part is "as written" when it's exactly what one of the styles writes over the chords it was written for (the chords before the edit, or the ones it was last written for this visit); anything else was changed by hand, is named in the question ("rewriting replaces your changes"), and is rewritten in the stack. **No saving of hand edits**: the rewrite is one Undo step (`arranger.history`'s `harmony` field: only the parts' text, `harmonyPartsText`, so undoing never undoes a melody edit made since); Undo's buttons are on Tracks only. A capo move is skipped (`setCapo` transposes the tune itself); while the chart can't be read, the last readable chords are kept to compare with. Not tried in the browser yet.
- Each part has its own mixer channel (id = part id, added to `mixer.channels` from `HARMONY_PARTS`, saved with the mix like any strip). `partChannels.js` is the one place that maps voices to channels: abcjs keeps only a voice's name (`staff.title`), so a voice named like a part plays on that part's channel, anything else on Melody. Every `MelodyNote`/`MelodyEvent` carries its `channel`; `band.js` connects by it, stems include a part's channel only when the song has it, and the MIDI file gets one track per voice (channels 4–8). The mixer shows a part's strip only while the song has it; removing a part clears its solo. The pickup is read from the first voice only.
- **Passing chords** (`harmonyStyles.js`, the newest style): the voices read what each melody note is doing (`harmonyRoles.js`, `theory/noteRoles.js`): on an approach note they sing a passing chord of their own (`theory/passingChords.js`: the dominant of the chord it leads to first), on an anticipation the chord it reaches early, and they hold through a suspension; target notes get the stack. The band's chords never change. Every other style ignores the roles (their lines checked identical). Not tried by ear yet.
- Ideas not built: a passing note held against a chord tone (oblique motion) instead of parallel; parts above the lead drawn above it (`%%score`); counterpoint.

### `follow.js`

- While playing, the sheet music follows the band: the sounding note gets the `abcjs-playing` class, an `abcjs-cursor` rect glides across the staff, and the page scrolls to a new line if it's out of view (only while the sheet music is on screen).
- Positions come from abcjs `TimingCallbacks(tune).noteTimings`, which lists notes as played (repeats expanded) with their SVG `elements`, `left`, `endX`, `top` and `height`. Its milliseconds are rescaled to quarter notes by proportion (total played quarters ÷ total ms), because abcjs's `qpm` counts the meter's beat, not quarters.
- Time comes from `playArrangement`'s `beats()` (the audio clock), mapped the same way `melody.js` times the melody (`melodyTime`), never from abcjs's own timer.

### `bassline.js`

- The band's own bass part: always root and fifth (usually the fifth below), one note per boom, each root in the octave nearest the last note. No style choice (the user's call: the Root and fifth / Active menu was removed). The walks and approaches into chord changes, the fills under the tune's long notes (`bassFills.js`, `bass/melodySpace.js`) and the style data (`bassStyles.js`) are in commit 022f85e if they're wanted back.
- Articulation is seeded (mulberry32), so exports are deterministic.
- Gates and velocities shape articulation.
- Tuning constants sit at the top of the file.
